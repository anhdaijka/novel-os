# Style Bible — Horrible

## Language

Vietnamese.

Use contemporary Vietnamese with a neutral-to-Northern register and natural Northern Vietnamese dialogue/social texture. Avoid forced dialect spelling unless a character-specific choice has been researched and approved.

## POV

Third-person limited centered primarily and closely on Khanh.

One consciousness per scene. No casual head-hopping.

Occasional documents, recordings, messages, testimony, diaries, oral retellings or other embedded artifacts may widen the information surface without turning the main narration omniscient.

## Tense / narrative stance

Use a natural present-tense-like Vietnamese narrative flow rather than overusing `đã`. Let aspect/time be clear from context and normal Vietnamese grammar.

## Narrative distance

Close enough to register Khanh's bodily fear, doubt, pattern-recognition and moral discomfort, but not so close that every observation becomes interior monologue.

## Core prose identity

Everyday on the surface. Spiritual unease under the skin. Investigation is one method Khanh uses to peel at those layers, not the novel's entire language.

Horror scenes should remain sensory and lived, not become forensic summaries. Investigative scenes should retain atmosphere, social discomfort and human stakes.

Core rules:

- **Describe the wrongness before naming the horror.**
- **Concrete first, interpretation second.**
- **Fear lives in interruption of normal patterns.**
- **The dead should feel like former people before they feel like supernatural objects.**

## Prose density

Medium by default. Slow-burn does not mean static. Each substantial scene should advance at least one of: clue, contradiction, dread, character, relationship, moral pressure, setting or consequence.

Use lyrical prose selectively, especially around memory, grief, apparition and uncanny stillness. Avoid making every paragraph metaphor-heavy.

## Description philosophy

Prefer concrete Vietnamese domestic, urban, market, roadside and workplace details over generic `dark / eerie / mysterious` abstraction.

A location should feel lived in before it becomes frightening.

Follow perception order rather than automatically describing a whole room first. Use a few functional sensory anchors rather than cataloguing every sense.

Use ordinary sensory anchors: market noise, motorbikes, incense, paper, fluorescent office light, rain, food, family clutter, phone notifications, corridor acoustics, road dust, humidity, old furniture, shrines, documents and shop inventory.

Do not make every uncanny location visibly gothic.

## Dialogue philosophy

Dialogue should sound like people with jobs, families and existing relationships, not lore delivery systems.

Characters may joke, evade, misremember, change subject, understate fear or use culturally familiar euphemism instead of naming supernatural things directly.

Family speech is practical and familiar. Mother is concrete and quick; father tends to speak less; the older sister is warm but practical rather than indulgent; the brother-in-law is civil and slightly stiff without default hidden conflict.

The hometown female friend is quick, practical and lightly teasing. The hometown joke for Khanh after his return is `Việt kiều Nhật`, not `Cán bộ Nhật`; use sparingly and naturally.

## Interior monologue

Khanh thinks like an engineer without narrating a programming textbook. He tends toward causal models, contradictions, reproducibility and root cause. His flaw is over-needing a clean explanation.

Do not convert reasoning into constant Sherlock-style deduction or bullet-list logic in prose.

Technical analogies may trigger insight, but life supplies the analogy while evidence supplies the answer.

## Technical prose

Use accurate, light technical language when work matters. Logs, timestamps, credentials, retries, APIs, deployment and similar detail should sound natural to engineers without becoming tutorial exposition.

Avoid code blocks unless unusually justified. Short logs/messages are preferable.

Japanese dialogue is normally rendered in Vietnamese; retain Japanese only when linguistic/social nuance matters.

## Horror language

Horror is not limited to hostile entities. Apparitions can be frightening because they are impossible, intimate, sad or unresolved.

Normality first, then one thing stops fitting.

Apparitions may look entirely ordinary. Do not default to grotesque design, cold spots, generic spine-chills or jumpscare grammar.

Express Khanh's fear through body response first: breath, muscle lock, hearing, dry mouth, hesitation, freezing, involuntary movement or refusal to look.

Do not explain away every supernatural encounter immediately after it happens. Let experienced fear remain emotionally real even when rational alternatives remain possible.

Some uncanny moments may never become clues.

## Embedded oral-horror / folklore layer

Stories told by people other than Khanh are a major horror and cultural channel, not decorative filler.

A customer, parent, market contact, elderly relative, ritual practitioner, colleague or other character may tell a complete small ghost story inside the novel. Such stories may be more openly folkloric, frightening, exaggerated or strange than Khanh's close-third narration because they inherit the teller's voice and beliefs.

A told story is **testimony**, not objective world truth. The teller may exaggerate, misremember, combine stories, repeat hearsay, hide details, sincerely believe something false or genuinely report something supernatural.

Therefore folklore claims and apparent supernatural abilities inside an oral story do not automatically bind main-world author canon.

Embedded stories may exist simply to:

- scare and entertain;
- preserve oral-storytelling texture;
- show Vietnamese spiritual/religious/folk-cultural beauty and complexity;
- expose darker human uses of belief such as exploitation, fear, pressure, fraud or mistaken certainty;
- introduce vocabulary, motifs or competing interpretations;
- create thematic resonance;
- occasionally bridge into a main case.

Not every embedded story needs payoff.

If a story later crosses into the main plot, its claims must pass through the normal evidence framework before becoming author-canon fact.

## Cultural voice and respect

The novel may actively help readers encounter the richness, beauty, contradictions and darker social edges of Vietnamese Buddhism, Đạo Mẫu / Thờ Mẫu, ancestor practices, local folk belief, ritual life, votive-paper culture, funeral/memorial practices and Northern Vietnamese folklore.

Do not turn living traditions into interchangeable magic systems or use them only as scary décor.

The darker side belongs to specific people, incentives, institutions or situations — fraud, manipulation, fear, commercialization, stigma, social pressure or mistaken belief — not to a lazy claim that Buddhism, Đạo Mẫu or Vietnamese folk religion is inherently sinister.

Folklore categories of ghosts, spirits, demons, corpse phenomena and possibly non-human beings are valuable story material, but named categories remain `folk belief` or `character belief` unless separately established at author level.

## Mystery language

Avoid repetitive procedural rhythm of clue → interview → timeline → culprit.

Investigation should happen inside homes, shops, workplaces, funerals, markets, messages, memories, family discomfort and ordinary obligations.

Mystery must sharpen the horror, never sterilize it.

The novel should contain substantial horror/spiritual material outside formal cases: oral ghost stories, rituals as lived culture, legends, family memories, direct uncanny experiences and incidents nobody investigates.

## Humor

Humor is welcome and necessary. Prefer relationship-based teasing, workplace dry humor, family practicality and local familiarity over punchline-writing.

Khanh may have dry humor but is not a quip machine.

## Emotional writing

Prefer routine, objects, food, habits, silence and unfinished gestures over explicit explanation after every emotional beat.

Grandfather memories may hold affection and fear at the same time; do not saintify him.

## Violence / intimacy treatment

Violence: low-to-moderate explicitness by default, with detail proportional to emotional and causal importance rather than spectacle.

Romance: no default love interest. Let relationships exist fully before deciding whether chemistry becomes romantic.

## Recurring motifs / imagery

Provisional, not mandatory:

- paper and ash
- names written on paper
- thresholds between busy public life and private rooms
- records left by the dead: notebooks, orders, accounts, logs, photographs, objects
- ordinary systems retaining traces of absent people
- practical light: shop bulbs, candles, fluorescent office lighting, phone screens
- crowds that can hide an uncanny presence more effectively than empty haunted spaces
- market noise and the sudden absence of expected noise
- familiar voices that are slightly wrong

## Chapter presentation

Numbered chapters are preferred. Short optional chapter titles may use an image/object/phrase such as `Đơn hàng`, `Ba người trong ảnh`, `Không nhận lễ` rather than announcing plot function.

Vary endings among image, decision, contradiction, relationship beat, ordinary action with changed meaning, quiet dread and occasional cliffhanger.

## Avoid

- generic AI abstraction
- redundant emotional explanation after an already clear action/image
- repetitive `không phải X, mà là Y` rhetoric
- mechanical rhetorical triads
- ending every scene with a quotable aphorism
- random literary metaphors unrelated to Khanh's life/world
- expository dialogue that characters would not naturally say
- melodramatic mystical NPC speech
- constant adjectives announcing fear
- every chapter ending on a jumpscare
- generic Western haunted-house language pasted onto Northern Vietnam
- technical jargon used only to show intelligence
- over-explaining familiar Vietnamese culture to Vietnamese characters/readers
- automatically framing faith as stupidity or skepticism as moral superiority
- treating every oral ghost story as literal canon
- reducing folklore to a monster/power catalogue

## Author voice references

The author values oral-horror intimacy, everyday causality, karmic / moral consequence, haunting aftertaste, foreshadowing, cultural texture and re-read value. Draw from these high-level craft properties rather than imitating any named storyteller's exact prose voice.
