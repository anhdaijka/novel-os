# Style Bible — Horrible

## Language

Vietnamese.

Natural contemporary Northern Vietnamese dialogue and social texture. Avoid forced dialect writing unless a character-specific choice has been researched and approved.

## POV

Third-person limited centered primarily on Khanh.

Occasional documents, recordings, messages, testimony, diaries or other embedded artifacts may widen the information surface without casually switching omniscient POV.

## Tense

TBD during later prose discovery. Do not infer a fixed tense from the framework template.

## Narrative distance

Close enough to register Khanh's bodily fear, doubt, pattern-recognition and moral discomfort, but not so close that every observation becomes interior monologue.

## Core prose identity

Everyday on the surface. Spiritual unease under the skin. Investigation is the method Khanh uses to peel at those layers.

Horror scenes should remain sensory and lived, not become forensic summaries. Investigative scenes should retain atmosphere, social discomfort and human stakes.

## Prose density

Medium by default. Slow-burn does not mean static. Each substantial scene should advance at least one of: clue, contradiction, dread, character, relationship, moral pressure, setting or consequence.

## Description philosophy

Prefer concrete Vietnamese domestic, urban, market, roadside and workplace details over generic “dark / eerie / mysterious” abstraction.

A location should feel lived in before it becomes frightening.

Use ordinary sensory anchors: market noise, motorbikes, incense, paper, fluorescent office light, rain, food, family clutter, phone notifications, corridor acoustics, road dust, humidity, old furniture, shrines, documents, shop inventory.

Do not make every uncanny location visibly gothic.

## Dialogue philosophy

Dialogue should sound like people with jobs, families and existing relationships, not lore delivery systems.

Characters may joke, evade, misremember, change subject, understate fear or use culturally familiar euphemism instead of naming supernatural things directly.

The hometown joke for Khanh after his return is “Việt kiều Nhật”, not “Cán bộ Nhật”. Use sparingly and naturally.

## Interior monologue

Khanh thinks like an engineer without narrating a programming textbook. He tends toward causal models, contradictions, reproducibility and root cause. His flaw is over-needing a clean explanation.

Technical analogies may trigger insight, but life supplies the analogy while evidence supplies the answer.

## Horror language

Horror is not limited to hostile entities. Apparitions can be frightening because they are impossible, intimate, sad or unresolved.

Do not explain away every supernatural encounter immediately after it happens. Let experienced fear remain emotionally real even when rational alternatives remain possible.

Some uncanny moments may never become clues.

## Mystery language

Avoid repetitive procedural rhythm of clue → interview → timeline → culprit.

Investigation should happen inside homes, shops, workplaces, funerals, markets, messages, memories, family discomfort and ordinary obligations.

Mystery must sharpen the horror, never sterilize it.

## Humor

Humor is welcome and necessary. Prefer relationship-based teasing, workplace dry humor, family practicality and local familiarity over punchline-writing.

## Violence / intimacy treatment

Violence: low-to-moderate explicitness by default, with detail proportional to emotional and causal importance.

Romance: no default love interest. Let relationships exist fully before deciding whether chemistry becomes romantic.

## Recurring motifs / imagery

Provisional, not mandatory:
- thresholds between busy public life and private rooms
- records left by the dead: notebooks, orders, accounts, logs, photographs, objects
- ordinary systems retaining traces of absent people
- light that is practical rather than gothic: shop bulbs, candles, fluorescent office lighting, phone screens
- crowds that can hide an uncanny presence more effectively than empty haunted spaces

## Avoid

- generic AI abstraction
- redundant emotional explanation after an already clear action/image
- expository dialogue that characters would not naturally say
- melodramatic mystical NPC speech
- constant adjectives announcing fear
- every chapter ending on a jumpscare
- generic Western haunted-house language pasted onto Northern Vietnam
- technical jargon used only to show intelligence
- automatically framing faith as stupidity or skepticism as moral superiority

## Author voice references

The author values oral-horror intimacy, everyday causality, karmic / moral consequence, haunting aftertaste, foreshadowing and re-read value. Draw from these high-level craft properties rather than imitating any named living or deceased storyteller's exact prose voice.
