# Creative Boundaries — Horrible

These are project-specific constraints for the `story/horrible-development` branch.

## Hard boundaries

- Do not turn the novel into detective procedural fiction with ghosts as decoration.
- Do not turn Khanh into a chosen one, hereditary sorcerer, omnipotent medium, demon hunter or escalating supernatural fighter without explicit future author approval.
- Do not let technical skill become magical access to police, telecom, banking or private databases.
- Do not let spiritual practitioners function as infallible exposition machines.
- Do not equate religious or folk practice with evil cultism by default.
- Real Vietnamese beliefs and living traditions should be researched respectfully; malicious occult systems should be fictionalized when appropriate.
- Do not convert personal family inspiration into literal biography unless the author explicitly chooses to do so for a specific element.
- Emotional truth may be inspired by life; plot facts, crimes, identities and family secrets may be transformed or fictionalized.

## Mystery boundaries

- A case may be solved, mostly solved, partially solved, unresolved, dormant, intentionally ambiguous or wrongly closed.
- Unresolved cases still require designed causal possibilities and evidence discipline.
- Coincidence may trigger attention but cannot become a lazy solution.
- Everyday-life insight may reframe existing evidence; it must not conjure missing evidence.
- Reader-facing clues should remain fair when a later reveal depends on them.
- Police and professional investigators must retain plausible competence. Khanh should not routinely outperform trained investigators at their own jobs.

## Supernatural boundaries

- Do not over-confirm cosmology early.
- Do not create rigid RPG-like rules, levels or power scaling.
- Apparitions, dreams, possession-like events and uncanny phenomena may be experienced strongly without a final metaphysical verdict.
- The dead are not automatically truthful, benevolent or omniscient.
- A human explanation can coexist with supernatural residue.
- A supernatural interpretation can be compelling without becoming authorial fact.
- Not every uncanny beat needs plot payoff.

## Tone boundaries

- Dread and atmosphere over constant jumpscares.
- Gore low-to-moderate by default; use when story consequence requires it, not as spectacle.
- Preserve ordinary humor, work, meals, errands, family calls and social friction as contrast.
- Avoid keeping every scene dark, solemn or mystical.

## Character outcomes requiring explicit author approval

- death of core recurring cast
- resurrection
- irreversible betrayal
- major romance commitment or breakup
- identity reveal that changes a core relationship
- confirmed supernatural ability
- permanent power or ability change
- Khanh abandoning software engineering to become a spiritual professional
- grandfather being revealed as a villain or master architect of the series

## Plot mechanisms to avoid by default

- secret agency that conveniently handles the supernatural
- inherited grimoire / ultimate ritual manual
- “all cases were connected to one ancient demon” shortcut
- prophecy that removes character agency
- dream as reliable oracle or cheat code
- every ghost scene delivering clues
- every apparent accident becoming murder
- CCTV / AI enhancement that produces impossible forensic certainty
- convenient police-friend database lookups
- escalating magical combat as the main resolution engine

## Scope boundaries

The long series can expand geographically and thematically, including later returns to Japan, but should stay grounded in human-scale horror, spirituality, memory, wrongdoing, grief, social life and investigation. Expansion must deepen the world rather than simply increase supernatural power levels.
