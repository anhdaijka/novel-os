# Discovery 10 — Narrative Voice, Prose & Embedded Folklore

Status: **accepted development material, not yet Story Skills canon**.

## Narrative voice

- Third-person limited, centered closely on Khanh.
- One consciousness per scene; no casual head-hopping.
- Occasional documents, testimony, diary, recording, message, notebook or other embedded artifact may widen the narrative surface.
- Vietnamese narration uses a natural present-tense-like flow rather than overusing `đã`.
- Narrative distance is close enough to register body response, doubt and pattern-recognition without converting every observation into internal monologue.

## Prose identity

- Contemporary Vietnamese with a neutral-to-Northern register.
- Medium prose density; long-form readability matters.
- Literary/lyrical language is selective and earned, strongest around memory, grief, apparition and uncanny stillness.
- Concrete sensory detail before abstract mood.
- Description follows Khanh's perception order rather than mandatory scenic establishing shots.
- Use a few functional sensory details rather than cataloguing every sense.
- Locations feel lived-in before they feel frightening.

Core rules:

> **Describe the wrongness before naming the horror.**

> **Concrete first, interpretation second.**

> **Fear lives in interruption of normal patterns.**

> **The dead should feel like former people before they feel like supernatural objects.**

## Horror prose

- Normality first; then one detail stops fitting.
- Apparitions may look entirely ordinary.
- Do not default to grotesque ghost design, cold spots, generic spine-chills or constant jumpscares.
- Khanh's fear is expressed through body behavior first: breath, hearing, muscle tension, freezing, avoidance, involuntary movement.
- Full apparitions are allowed but remain rare enough to matter.
- Ghost speech should usually be restrained; one line can carry more weight than a lore monologue.
- Horror scenes do not need to end with a scare. Ordinary sound returning, morning arriving or a quiet image may end the scene.
- Gore remains low-to-moderate and proportional to consequence rather than spectacle.

## Dialogue

- Family dialogue is practical, familiar and often task-oriented.
- Mother speaks quickly and concretely; father tends to speak less and avoid exposition.
- Older sister is warm but practical, not indulgent.
- Brother-in-law is civil and slightly stiff, without a hidden feud by default.
- Hometown female friend is quick, practical and lightly teasing; `Việt kiều Nhật` is a recurring joke used sparingly.
- Workplace dialogue uses accurate technical language but does not explain basic engineering concepts to prove intelligence.
- Japanese dialogue is normally rendered in Vietnamese, with original Japanese retained only when social or linguistic nuance materially matters.
- Dialogue may include contemporary slang/profanity naturally, without using it as a shortcut for realism.

## Interior reasoning

- Khanh thinks in causal models, contradictions, reproducibility and root cause.
- Do not turn his reasoning into constant Sherlock-style deduction or bullet-list logic in prose.
- His engineering habits are character texture and a source of analogy, not supernatural competence.
- `Life supplies the analogy; evidence supplies the answer.`

## Humor and emotional writing

- Humor is moderate, relational and ordinary: family banter, dry workplace humor, teasing between old acquaintances.
- Do not Marvelize Khanh into a quip machine.
- Grief is expressed through routine, objects, food, memory, absence and unfinished habits more often than speeches.
- Do not explain emotion after an image/action already communicates it.
- Grandfather memories may be affectionate and frightening at once; do not saintify him.

## Anti-generic / anti-AI prose rules

Avoid as defaults:

- abstract emotional labels such as `một nỗi sợ nguyên thủy` or `bầu không khí trở nên nặng nề` when concrete experience can carry the scene;
- repetitive `không phải X, mà là Y` constructions;
- rhetorical triads used mechanically;
- every scene/chapter ending in a quotable aphorism;
- random literary metaphors unrelated to Khanh's life and vocabulary;
- over-explaining familiar Vietnamese culture to Vietnamese characters/readers;
- lore-delivery dialogue;
- every chapter ending `rồi anh nhìn thấy...`;
- technical jargon as status display;
- gothic-Western haunted-house language pasted over Northern Vietnamese settings.

Metaphor should emerge naturally from Khanh's world: systems, roads, logistics, paper, ash, electricity, screens, rain, records and work.

## Motif language

Strong recurring motifs may include:

- paper and ash;
- names written on paper;
- shop orders, old notebooks, logs, accounts and records;
- thresholds and doors;
- market noise and sudden absence of expected noise;
- fluorescent light, phone screens and ordinary artificial light;
- familiar voices that are slightly wrong;
- systems retaining traces of people who are gone.

## Chapter presentation

- Numbered chapters are preferred.
- Short optional titles may use an object/image/phrase rather than announcing plot function, e.g. `Đơn hàng`, `Ba người trong ảnh`, `Không nhận lễ`.
- Chapter endings should vary among image, decision, contradiction, quiet dread, changed ordinary action, relationship beat and occasional cliffhanger.

# Embedded Folklore / Oral-Horror Layer

The novel deliberately uses stories told **by people other than Khanh** as a major horror and cultural channel.

This is not optional garnish. It is part of the novel's reader promise.

## Purpose

Embedded stories can:

1. preserve and celebrate the richness of Vietnamese spiritual, religious and folk-cultural life;
2. expose darker social practices, exploitation, fear, stigma, fraud, family pressure and misuse of belief without condemning an entire tradition;
3. let readers encounter ghost stories, beliefs and supernatural categories beyond Khanh's direct experience;
4. create horror variety without forcing every story into a formal investigation;
5. preserve an oral-storytelling feeling: someone remembers, exaggerates, warns, jokes, misremembers or insists something happened;
6. plant bridges, motifs, terminology or partial information that may later matter to the main plot;
7. keep `Horrible` horror/spiritual-first rather than reducing it to investigative procedural fiction.

## Reliability rule

A story told by a character is **testimony**, not objective world truth.

The teller may:

- exaggerate;
- confuse chronology;
- combine two stories;
- repeat hearsay as personal knowledge;
- reinterpret an ordinary event through belief;
- hide embarrassing details;
- add a frightening ending because that is how the story is commonly told;
- genuinely believe something false;
- genuinely report something supernatural.

Therefore an embedded ghost story is allowed to contain apparent supernatural abilities or folklore rules that are **not** binding on main-world author canon.

If an embedded story later crosses into the main plot, its claims must be reclassified through the normal evidence system:

- observed event;
- witnesses;
- physical evidence;
- natural models;
- folk interpretation;
- supernatural models;
- private author status.

## Cultural function

The novel should give space to the beauty, complexity, history, community meaning and contradictions of Vietnamese traditions, especially where relevant to Northern Vietnam:

- Phật giáo and local Buddhist life;
- Đạo Mẫu / Thờ Mẫu and associated living practice;
- ancestor and family ritual;
- village đình/đền/chùa/phủ/miếu contexts;
- votive-paper economy and beliefs around offerings;
- funeral, memorial and grave practices;
- local specialists, mediums, diviners and ritual practitioners;
- oral ghost lore and local warnings;
- folk concepts of ghosts, wandering spirits, resentful dead and possibly non-human presences.

The novel may show both beauty and darkness:

- devotion, music, color, generosity, community, memory, mourning and care;
- exploitation, performance, fraud, fear, social pressure, superstition used as control, commercial incentives and people making harmful decisions from sincere but mistaken belief.

The darker side belongs to people, institutions, incentives and specific situations. It must not be lazily presented as proof that Buddhism, Đạo Mẫu or Vietnamese folk belief is inherently sinister.

## Folklore creatures and categories

Northern Vietnamese folk concepts of types of ghosts, spirits, demons, corpse phenomena and other supernatural beings are valuable story material.

Rules:

- Research the actual term, geography, source tradition and variation before presenting it as cultural information.
- A named folklore category is normally `folk belief` or `character belief`, not automatically author-canon species taxonomy.
- Different people may use the same word differently.
- Contradictory explanations are useful and culturally believable.
- Folklore categories can seed standalone oral tales, case hypotheses, warnings, mistaken diagnoses or genuine supernatural possibilities.
- Do not convert the corpus into a game bestiary or fixed weakness/power chart.

## Embedded-story formats

Possible forms include:

- mother/customer story at the shop;
- a meal-table story;
- hometown friend retelling something from the market/logistics network;
- an elderly relative's memory;
- a ritual practitioner's cautionary case;
- a monk/nun or temple/pagoda-context account when researched and contextually appropriate;
- a workplace colleague's hometown story;
- voice message / call / recording;
- local online post or sensational retelling;
- contradictory versions from several people;
- a remembered childhood story from Khanh's family;
- stories attached to travel into another Northern region.

Not every embedded tale needs payoff. Some exist to widen the spiritual world, scare the reader, reveal worldview, preserve oral culture or create thematic resonance.

## Bridge rule

An embedded story may become a bridge to the main narrative in three ways:

1. **Motif bridge** — introduces a concept/image later echoed in a case.
2. **Knowledge bridge** — gives Khanh vocabulary or a hypothesis, not an answer.
3. **Causal bridge** — a person/place/object from the tale later becomes directly relevant.

Coincidental embedded tales must not solve mysteries by themselves.

## Tone rule

Embedded tales may be more openly frightening, folkloric, exaggerated or strange than Khanh's close-third main narration because they inherit the teller's voice and belief.

That difference is desirable.

The reader should sometimes enjoy a complete small ghost story inside the larger novel without needing Khanh to investigate it.

## Genre balance

The novel is not `detective fiction plus supernatural residue`.

A healthy volume should contain horror/spiritual material outside formal cases:

- oral ghost stories;
- culturally grounded practices;
- family memories;
- strange incidents with no investigation;
- legends and competing explanations;
- rituals observed as social/religious life;
- direct uncanny experiences;
- grief and relationship to the dead.

Mystery remains one engine, not the whole language of the series.
