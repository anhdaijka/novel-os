## Short Haunting 03 — working title `Người về ăn giỗ`

This arc remains a **short spiritual haunting**, not a third investigative case.

### 1. Deceased person

Default accepted shell: an older woman from a normal local family, dead for roughly 6–8 years.

- No unresolved murder is required.
- She was not a saint and not a monster.
- Her death is ordinary enough that the arc cannot turn into another crime puzzle.
- The emotional center is memory, family estrangement and a small unfinished duty.

Exact name, age, kinship map and cause of death remain for scene-level design/research rather than further discovery rounds.

### 2. Why Khanh is present

Lan has a normal commercial/family reason to send or bring votive/custom goods to a household preparing a death-anniversary gathering. Khanh is home for a weekend after Case 02 and helps with delivery or transport.

No one seeks him because he is believed to solve ghosts.

### 3. Household belief split

The family does not live in panic.

- one person believes the deceased sometimes returns around the anniversary;
- another treats the stories as grief, habit or coincidence;
- other relatives may be indifferent, embarrassed or mildly superstitious.

No one has a unified supernatural interpretation.

### 4. The unfinished promise/duty

Before dying, the woman asked a descendant to return a small collection of personal things to an estranged sister/relative after a long family rupture.

Working objects:
- a small box of photographs;
- several letters or papers;
- one ordinary personal item if needed.

The objects are not cursed, valuable or magical.

The promise remained unfulfilled because the surviving family did not want to reopen the estrangement.

### 5. Causality remains unproven

The family may connect recurring anniversary phenomena to the unfulfilled promise, but the author does not certify that causal model.

Completing the promise can be emotionally right without proving why the strange experiences occurred.

### 6. Khanh's direct apparition-like experience

During ordinary anniversary preparation, with the house busy and people moving naturally, Khanh sees an older woman walk from the yard/threshold into an interior space.

Key handling:
- she looks ordinary;
- no corpse makeup, translucence, white-gown coding, impossible movement or jump scare;
- Khanh may instinctively shift aside to let her pass;
- the event lasts only seconds;
- he initially assumes she is a living relative or guest.

This should be one of Volume 1's strongest direct uncanny experiences precisely because nothing about the figure initially looks supernatural.

### 7. Why the event becomes difficult to naturalize

Khanh later asks who the woman was and describes one specific, mundane detail he had no plausible reason to know.

Preferred type of detail:
- an old hand/arm stiffness or habitual gesture after a past injury;
- a distinctive way of carrying or tying a scarf/cloth;
- another embodied habit known inside the family.

Use **one** high-value detail, not a stack of impossible facts.

Do not repeat Case Zero's photograph-recognition mechanism.

### 8. No camera rescue

There is no useful CCTV or phone footage of the apparition.

This arc deliberately deprives Khanh of the verification systems used in Case Zero and Case 02.

Natural alternatives can remain:
- another living older woman was present and later forgotten/misidentified;
- Khanh's memory was contaminated by later conversation;
- crowd movement/attention produced a mistaken perception;
- the identifying detail was indirectly available to him without his noticing.

None needs to fully satisfy him.

### 9. Khanh refuses to turn it into a case

He asks enough to understand whom his description resembles and what the family thinks happened.

He does **not**:
- build a witness timeline;
- demand photographs from everyone;
- search for a hidden culprit;
- summon a practitioner to diagnose the event;
- treat the house as a paranormal site.

This restraint is character development, not lack of curiosity.

### 10. The human action

Khanh's useful contribution is simple:

If someone promised to return the deceased woman's things, that promise remains worth honoring regardless of whether the apparition was supernatural.

He may say some version of the reasoning, but does not need a thematic speech.

The surviving relative makes the final decision and carries out the act. Khanh is not a ritual hero or mediator who fixes the family.

### 11. After completion

No `spirit released` confirmation.

Possible outcome:
- no further phenomenon is reported that anniversary/year;
- family interprets this according to existing beliefs;
- Khanh records only what happened and refuses to turn correlation into proof.

No glowing departure, final ghost smile, karmic scoreboard or narrator certification of `siêu thoát`.

### 12. Grandfather overlap

Later, while reviewing old notebooks, Khanh may find an entry containing the same locality, family name or related household reference with terse wording such as:

`Không xem.`

It must remain unclear whether:
- the entry concerns this deceased woman;
- it concerns another member of the same family;
- it concerns a totally unrelated matter in the same place.

The overlap exists only to contribute to the Volume-ending refusal pattern. It is not proof that grandfather predicted or understood the haunting.

## Short Haunting author-side truth matrix

### Objective human facts

- An older woman died 6–8 years before the arc.
- She left an unfulfilled request involving personal items and an estranged relative.
- Her family has accumulated small stories around anniversary dates.
- Khanh is present through ordinary family/shop logistics.
- Khanh reports seeing an older woman move through the house.
- His description includes one high-value embodied detail associated with the deceased.
- The promised items are eventually returned.
- No further event is reported afterward within the Volume.

### Character beliefs

Possible household models include:
- she comes home for the anniversary;
- she cannot leave because the promise is unfinished;
- relatives are reading grief/coincidence as a sign;
- stories have accumulated and contaminated memory.

These are beliefs, not author truth.

### Natural models

Keep at least these viable at design level:

1. **Misidentification:** Khanh saw another older woman and later matched memory incorrectly.
2. **Memory contamination:** post-event family discussion altered the remembered gesture/detail.
3. **Unnoticed prior exposure:** Khanh had already encountered the distinctive detail through conversation/object/context without encoding it consciously.
4. **Attention/crowd error:** anniversary preparation created a short perceptual mistake.

No natural model must be fully satisfying.

### Supernatural model

The deceased woman genuinely appeared briefly during the anniversary gathering, possibly connected to unfinished attachment or duty.

Even if this is author-private preferred interpretation, the text should not publish a mechanism, ability set or proof that completing the promise caused the apparition to stop.

### Author status

Default status: **strongly implied / deliberately not mechanized**.

The event should move Khanh's personal confidence toward `something genuinely happened` without converting the novel into objective cosmology.

## Volume 1 Ending Truth Matrix

### Reader can know with high confidence by the end

- Case Zero's present-day death has a plausible human/accidental mechanism.
- A buried family truth explains much of Case Zero's human pressure.
- The identity of the woman who placed the votive order remains unresolved.
- Case 02 contains real human credential misuse.
- Case 02 also leaves a later technical residue not satisfactorily attributed.
- Khanh has now experienced at least one direct event in `Người về ăn giỗ` that he personally finds very difficult to naturalize.
- Grandfather was not infallible and sometimes refused, failed or did not know.
- Grandfather's notebooks contain repeated refusal language across apparently unrelated matters.
- Case 02 is **not** present in grandfather's records and must not be retrofitted into prophecy.
- Khanh begins a private evidence-oriented record of strange matters.

### Reader should still not know

- whether all refusal entries share one cause;
- whether one supernatural entity/pattern connects the cases;
- why grandfather refused specific matters;
- whether he refused from fear, ethics, ignorance, prior failure, danger, social boundaries or mixed reasons;
- how communication from the dead works;
- whether the apparition was objectively the deceased woman;
- whether Khanh is unusually sensitive;
- whether unusual stories truly cluster around Khanh or whether network/attention/selection bias explains the pattern.

### Author does not need to lock yet

- final afterlife cosmology;
- one master explanation for all refusal notes;
- a mastermind or common entity;
- supernatural species taxonomy;
- exact long-series answer to `Ai đang nói?`.

Preserve design room.

## Volume 1 ending action

Khanh does not announce a mission.

He begins a simple digital note/table with fields such as:
- name/title;
- date;
- source;
- what seems certain;
- what remains uncertain.

He records Case Zero and Case 02, and may add the anniversary event without ranking it as a `case`.

The parallel with grandfather's notebooks should be visible to the reader but not framed as destiny, inheritance of office or `nối nghiệp`.

End on ordinary action/image rather than an aphoristic quote-card line.
