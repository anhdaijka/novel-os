# Case 02 Development Brief — “Tài khoản của người chết”

Status: accepted development candidate. Not Story Skills canon yet.

## Purpose in series

Case 02 proves that `Horrible` is not limited to the family votive-paper shop or hometown folklore. The uncanny can enter Khanh's ordinary modern working life in Hanoi.

It should feel technologically credible while remaining horror / spiritual fiction first, not cybersecurity procedural fiction.

## Entry point

After Case Zero, Khanh returns to normal life for roughly 2–4 meaningful chapters:

- commute;
- office re-entry;
- project handover;
- workplace relationships;
- family calls;
- sister asking for practical favors;
- hometown friend messaging or joking;
- ordinary bugs, meetings and deadlines.

The next mystery forms out of a real production / security incident rather than a ghost story somebody brings to Khanh.

## Core incident

A credential associated with an engineer who died several months earlier appears in a production-system action.

The engineer's death was believed to be an ordinary traffic accident.

The credential should already have been disabled under proper offboarding / deceased-employee handling, so its appearance indicates a real process or security problem before anyone considers a supernatural explanation.

The first action should be mundane, such as configuration access or a normal technical operation, not a message from beyond the grave.

## Khanh's legitimate role

Khanh becomes involved because the incident touches systems he now supports or understands technically.

The company handles the incident through appropriate security / management channels. Khanh does not secretly take corporate evidence home or behave like a cyber vigilante.

His technical competence is relevant to logs, system behavior, account lifecycle, deployment history and engineering habits.

## Human mystery

Most of the suspicious activity should eventually receive a human explanation.

Possible accepted direction:

A living employee or other authorized actor has used / inherited / recovered the dead engineer's credential or identity because they are trying to retrieve, conceal or restore something the engineer left behind.

The material can include:

- logs;
- notes;
- recordings;
- complaint drafts;
- evidence of workplace misconduct;
- abandoned project data.

The old material creates moral / institutional pressure without requiring that the dead engineer's traffic death secretly become murder.

## Personal technical signature

One suspicious action resembles a habit associated with the deceased engineer:

- naming convention;
- typo;
- command ordering;
- comment habit;
- favorite file / dashboard path.

This is not proof of the supernatural. A living colleague could know and imitate the habit.

## Human resolution

The active credential misuse / access problem is substantially explained.

The company can remediate the real security weakness.

The people involved still have ordinary motives and consequences.

Khanh should not end the case by announcing a ghost explanation to the company.

## Uncanny residue

After the main credential issue is supposedly closed, one smaller event remains hard to reproduce or attribute.

Preferred shape:

- access occurs after revocation / rotation, or inside a system the identified human actor did not know existed;
- the event merely opens a file, dashboard or familiar resource heavily associated with the deceased engineer;
- no “I am dead” message, prophecy or direct accusation appears;
- plausible technical alternatives remain: stale credentials, delayed automation, timestamp behavior, cache, undocumented jobs, forgotten integration.

The important feeling is that a person who is gone may still leave an active trace inside systems designed to reduce every action to an identity and timestamp.

## Horror language

Do not let log analysis drain the uncanny feeling.

The workplace can be disturbing precisely because it is ordinary:

- fluorescent light late at night;
- a silent office seat belonging to someone no longer there;
- old chat threads where the dead employee still has an avatar;
- automated birthday / anniversary reminders;
- pull-request comments written in an ordinary tone shortly before death;
- systems that do not understand absence and continue to surface the dead as active users.

The haunting may be emotional, institutional and digital before it is metaphysical.

## Relationship to Case Zero

No direct conspiracy connection.

Do not use grandfather's notebook, the Case Zero family, a repeated occult symbol or a shared entity.

The thematic rhyme is sufficient:

- Case Zero explores family and ritual systems retaining traces of the dead.
- Case 02 explores digital and institutional systems retaining traces of the dead.

At this stage the only obvious common denominator is Khanh.

This creates a long-term question without answering it:

> Are strange things beginning to happen around Khanh, or has Khanh simply learned to notice what was always there?

## Khanh development

Khanh should initially reject supernatural framing more strongly here than in Case Zero because this is his professional domain.

A colleague joking “ghost account” may irritate him because he has real technical explanations to test.

The case teaches him that modern systems can produce uncertainty too. Technical data can be incomplete, inherited, delayed, duplicated or dependent on hidden assumptions.

This should deepen rather than weaken his rigor.

## No early Japan connection

The Japanese-side counterpart and Khanh's strange onsite memories remain part of his ordinary background. Do not reveal Case 02 to be caused by a Japanese spirit, old Japan case or hidden connection to his onsite years.
