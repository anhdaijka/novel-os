# Discovery 08 — Northern Vietnam Setting & Research Bible

Status: **accepted development decision**.

## Core setting

- Contemporary mid-2020s Northern Vietnam, internally tracked on a 2026-equivalent timeline without requiring the narration to announce the exact year early.
- Khanh's hometown is fictionalized at local level while using real higher-level geography when useful.
- Preferred geographic base: the former Bắc Giang side of present-day Bắc Ninh, in a transition zone between lowland Kinh Bắc and northern midlands.
- Travel to Hanoi should feel plausible within roughly 1–2 hours depending on route and traffic.
- The hometown is semi-urbanizing rather than a preserved old village: old residential lanes, market, remaining fields, low hills, waterways, cemeteries, warehouses, newer roads, industrial development and changing land use coexist.

## Khanh's home and market crossroads

- The family house and votive-paper shop sit on a main road immediately beside a busy market crossroads.
- The market begins activity before dawn. Produce and wholesale movement may begin around 03:00–04:00, followed by retail traffic later in the morning.
- This creates an important horror rule: hearing people or vehicles at night is often normal. The uncanny becomes stronger when ordinary explanation survives longer.
- Khanh's hometown friend works logistics near this crossroads. Her family's large vegetable/food supply business gives her operational local knowledge rather than magical gossip access.
- No single market elder or gossip figure knows everything; information is distributed across overlapping social networks.

## Votive-paper economy

- Khanh's family mainly sells retail goods, receives custom orders and does limited finishing/customization rather than manufacturing everything from raw material.
- Generic goods are sourced from wholesalers; one realistic supply direction is the Song Hồ / Đông Hồ commercial area.
- Real production centers remain ordinary places of work, not fictional occult capitals.
- Khanh may have accompanied family members on supply trips when younger and can occasionally help now.
- Contemporary votive goods can include traditional paper offerings as well as models of modern consumer objects, but exact inventory, ritual use and regional terminology must be researched before scene-level claims are treated as factual.

## Opening season

- Case Zero opens in late winter / early spring, around Rằm tháng Giêng.
- Preferred sensory weather: damp cold, drizzle, nồm, gray sky, wet pavement, crowded ritual-season commerce and spring festival traffic.
- Do not open the entire novel on Rằm tháng Bảy merely because it is a ghost story; save more obvious ghost-season associations for later use if needed.
- Seasonal change should be visible across the long series. Time passes through spring, summer heat/storms, autumn and winter rather than remaining in a permanent horror season.

## Living religious and folk traditions

- Đình, đền, chùa, phủ and miếu are not interchangeable labels.
- Buddhist clergy, folk ritual specialists, fortune tellers, thanh đồng/cô đồng and family ritual knowledge are not one generic spiritual profession.
- Real living traditions may contain admirable, ordinary, mistaken, exploitative or morally mixed practitioners because practitioners are people; the tradition itself must not be reduced to evil-cult shorthand.
- Real religious/cultural sites may be used when researched respectfully for travel, history and social texture.
- Murder, secret cult, malicious occult systems, hidden chambers and major invented scandals should default to fictional sites and institutions unless the author explicitly chooses otherwise after research.
- Thờ Mẫu and other living practices are cultural/religious traditions, not automatically a magic system for the novel.

## Funeral and ancestor-practice rule

- Do not assume one universal Northern Vietnamese funeral system.
- Funeral, memorial, cremation/burial, grave movement, anniversary and ancestor practices vary by family, locality, religion, generation and economic condition.
- Modernization and changing burial practices are part of the setting and can generate real social conflict without requiring the supernatural.
- Any specific practice such as 49-day, 100-day, giỗ, cải táng or local taboo must be researched for the relevant family and place before being treated as cultural fact.

## Cultural soundscape

- Quan họ and Kinh Bắc festival culture are first social and cultural realities, not paranormal devices.
- Music, festival sound and oral tradition may contribute atmosphere without being turned into a cursed-song trope by default.
- Khanh's hometown does not need to claim status as a historic Quan họ village.

## Modernity is equally important

The series setting must hold both old and new systems at once:

- shrine / factory
- cemetery / industrial or residential expansion
- village road / expressway
- oral memory / social media
- family genealogy / digital database
- market stall / logistics warehouse
- ancestral story / corporate identity record

Urban and industrial horror are valid parts of the same world: rented rooms, factories, loading yards, offices, hospitals, warehouses and parking areas can be as important as temples or graveyards.

## Regional expansion

- Khanh may travel beyond the Kinh Bắc core as the series expands.
- Nearby northern routes such as Lạng Sơn / Thái Nguyên and other midland or mountain areas are natural early expansion directions.
- Later travel can reach Cao Bằng, Hà Giang, Tuyên Quang, Lào Cai, Bắc Kạn and elsewhere when a case naturally requires it.
- Ethnic-minority settings are high-research zones. Never write a generic `người dân tộc` culture. Identify the actual community, geography, language/religious context and source basis.
- Real folklore and ritual traditions must not be harvested into a monster catalogue.

## Research protocol

For cultural or spiritual material, separate these layers:

1. **Research fact** — supported by credible sources.
2. **Community / oral testimony** — something a person or group says or believes.
3. **Character belief** — what a fictional character believes.
4. **Observed phenomenon** — what actually occurs on-page.
5. **Author-canon rule** — what the fictional universe has deliberately established as true.

Do not merge these categories.

Preferred source order:

1. community/primary or institutional sources;
2. academic research;
3. reputable journalism;
4. oral testimony;
5. internet folklore / ghost-audio / entertainment sources.

Lower-ranked sources are valuable for discovering questions, imagery and story possibilities but do not automatically establish cultural fact.

When fictionalizing from real practice, record:

- REAL BASIS
- FICTIONAL DEPARTURE
- WHY
- READER RISK

This prevents later agents from treating invented ritual as documentary fact.

## Sensory bible

### Visual palette

Avoid generic black-red gothic coding. Prefer ordinary Northern Vietnamese visual reality:

- fluorescent white light;
- colorful votive paper;
- gray ash;
- damp walls;
- wet tile;
- yellow road lighting;
- cold gray-blue drizzle;
- ritual red used amid normal domestic/commercial color.

### Smell

Possible recurring environmental smells include paper, incense, ash/smoke, vegetables and market produce, raw meat, cooking oil, vehicle exhaust, rain, damp concrete and breakfast food.

Do not make incense smell an automatic proof of supernatural presence.

### Sound

Normal hometown soundscape can include produce trucks before dawn, shutters, chopping from the market, vendors calling, loudspeakers, motorbikes, dogs, phone notifications, distant ritual/festival music and rain on sheet metal.

Silence matters because the home is normally noisy. A sudden absence of market sound can be more disturbing than a scream.

## Core rule

**Culture is not supernatural evidence.**

A ritual does not prove a ghost exists. A temple is not automatically haunted. A belief system is not a magic system. The world should feel culturally real first; the abnormal becomes frightening because it intrudes into a believable living environment.
