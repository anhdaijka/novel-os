# Discovery 09 — Supernatural Epistemology & Horror Rules

Status: **accepted development material**. This document is the detailed operational source of truth for supernatural phenomena in `Horrible`. It refines D-013 in `author/decisions.md` without turning the setting into a fantasy ruleset.

## Core epistemic rule

Always distinguish:

1. **Folk / cultural belief** — what a tradition or community says.
2. **Character belief / interpretation** — what a specific person thinks happened.
3. **Observed phenomenon** — what witnesses or instruments actually record.
4. **Author status** — what the project privately treats as confirmed, strongly implied, deliberately ambiguous, or unknown.

Narration is close third-person around Khanh and should normally report experience rather than certify labels such as `vong`, `ma`, `quỷ`, `căn`, or `nhập` as objective fact.

The series-level world is compatible with genuine supernatural reality and should eventually make `everything was hallucination` an unconvincing total explanation, but cosmology remains incomplete.

## The dead

- Death does not make a person benevolent, evil, truthful, omniscient, wise, or morally purified.
- A dead person may retain recognizable memory/personality continuity, but memory, awareness and identity need not be complete or stable.
- The dead do not automatically know events that happened after death.
- A murdered person only knows the killer if they had a basis to know; death does not grant retrospective omniscience.
- Do not establish mind-reading as a general dead-person ability.
- A dead person may lie, omit, manipulate, misunderstand, protect someone, protect their own reputation, seek revenge, or pursue an unresolved goal.
- A person can be both victim and morally compromised.
- A good person can produce a terrifying phenomenon without becoming morally corrupt.
- Some apparitions may behave as if they do not know they are dead; this is case material, not a universal rule.
- Do not answer whether consciousness persists forever or define a measurable `siêu thoát` state.
- `Siêu thoát`, `oan`, `vong`, etc. may be meaningful character/traditional language without becoming narrator-visible status flags.
- Solving a case does not guarantee a haunting stops; a haunting stopping does not prove a theory correct.

## Dreams and sleep states

- Dreams are low-confidence evidence, not oracle access.
- Dreams must never provide a clean mystery solution as a cheat code.
- A dream may contain information the dreamer apparently should not know; that is strongly uncanny evidence, not automatic proof.
- Check memory contamination, prior exposure, hearsay, coincidence and reconstruction before elevating a dream.
- Multiple people may have overlapping dreams, but exact copy-paste dreams are not the default; small specific overlaps are more believable and disturbing.
- Sleep paralysis receives serious physiological/psychological consideration.
- Khanh's strange Japan experiences may plausibly include episodes he himself interprets as sleep paralysis, stress or nightmare.

## Apparitions

- Full apparitions are allowed. The novel must not avoid visible ghosts so aggressively that the supernatural becomes only peripheral shadows.
- Rarely, Khanh may see a dead person clearly enough to experience the encounter as real.
- Apparitions need not be transparent, decomposed or visually monstrous; looking ordinary can be more disturbing.
- Physical interaction is not a universal apparition ability. Object movement, touch or injury must be established case by case.
- Camera visibility has no universal answer. A camera seeing something does not certify `ghost`; a camera failing to see something does not disprove a witness.
- Electronic devices are evidence systems with faults, delays, automation and artifacts; do not canonize them as spiritually sensitive instruments.

## Sensory manifestations

- Sound, smell, temperature, animal behavior and visual anomalies may participate in supernatural scenes, but none is a universal detector.
- `Mùi hương = có vong`, `chó sủa = có ma`, `lạnh = hiện diện` and similar shortcuts are prohibited as author rules.
- Establish normal soundscape first; absence or distortion of ordinary sound can be more frightening than a loud supernatural cue.

## Objects and places

- Phenomena may be associated with an object without proving that the object literally contains a spirit.
- Possible links include prior owner, memory, place of origin, a human relationship, ritual meaning or circumstances of transfer.
- Cursed objects are possible but extremely rare and should not become a franchise structure.
- Place hauntings are allowed, but keep open whether the phenomenon is tied to location, visitors, history or expectation.
- Repeated reports across generations are major-case material.
- Characters may believe in spiritually charged locations; the author does not maintain an objective `âm khí map` by default.

## Possession-like phenomena

Write phenomenology before taxonomy:

- behavioral change;
- voice or language change;
- information apparently unavailable to the subject;
- memory gaps;
- bodily behavior;
- social context;
- independent evidence.

Then permit competing models:

- deliberate performance or fraud;
- psychiatric / neurological / sleep / substance explanation where appropriate;
- dissociation or psychological crisis;
- culturally mediated behavior;
- genuine possession-like supernatural event;
- source identity different from the one characters assume.

Do not stigmatize mental illness as proof of supernatural influence.

A possession-like event may become strongly supernatural without narrator certification. The possessing source is not automatically a dead human. This preserves the later-series question: **Ai đang nói?**

## Corpse-reanimation folklore

- `Quỷ nhập tràng` and related corpse-reanimation stories may appear as rare major-horror material.
- Avoid zombie-like running, combat spectacle and creature taxonomy.
- A corpse movement must first take physical/postmortem possibilities seriously.
- If an event exceeds those explanations, keep the presentation restrained, bodily disturbing and difficult to classify.

## Possibly non-human presences

- The possibility of something that was never human may exist.
- Volume 1 does not need to confirm such beings.
- Narration should not classify species early. `Quỷ` or similar labels belong to characters unless later evidence justifies more.
- Human-ghost horror usually carries biography, grievance, memory or relationship. A possibly non-human presence is frightening partly because those human anchors may be absent.

## Curses, ritual and efficacy

- Curses may exist as beliefs and some cases may strongly suggest causal patterns, but there is no programmable spell mechanic.
- Do not establish `hatred alone always curses` or any universal causal formula.
- Ritual may appear to affect events, but efficacy is not an API and repeat performance need not produce identical results.
- Practitioners possess tradition, training, experience, interpretation and bias — not infallibility.
- Highly experienced practitioners can still be wrong.
- Khanh's grandfather was sometimes wrong. A future reason for some refusals may be that experience taught him the limits and costs of intervention.
- Ritual failure does not automatically mean incompetence; the diagnosis or interpretation may have been wrong.
- Ritual/prayer can have social, emotional and spiritual meaning for the living regardless of measurable paranormal effect.
- Do not establish `giờ âm`, auspicious/inauspicious times, or ritual calendars as universal objective mechanics without case-specific research.

## Sensitivity, `căn`, children and the dying

- `Children see ghosts more easily`, `the dying become sensitive`, `people with căn perceive more`, etc. remain folk/character hypotheses unless a later case earns stronger status.
- Khanh is not confirmed to have `căn`.
- Frequency of cases is not evidence that Khanh is chosen or supernaturally magnetic.
- A family member may give Khanh a protective object; whether it works or mainly reassures the living can remain ambiguous.
- Khanh may learn etiquette, context and how to assist around rituals. He does not acquire a ritual skill tree.
- If he participates in a ritual, he is normally a family member, helper, witness or participant rather than an instant ritual specialist.

## Harm and death

- Direct supernatural killing is possible in principle but extremely rare.
- Frequent arbitrary ghost-killing would destroy investigation, moral complexity and everyday realism.
- More often, supernatural or apparently supernatural pressure can contribute through a causal chain: fear, exhaustion, disorientation, panic, dangerous choices or perceptual error.
- Rare unexplained physical injuries are allowed and should be treated as serious evidence.
- Exceptional cases may exceed the usual causal-chain pattern, but such escalation must be deliberate and tracked.

## Karma and morality

- Nhân quả is not an observable supernatural scoreboard.
- Bad people may die peacefully; good people may suffer.
- The universe does not promise visible fairness.
- The project is not nihilistic: people still create moral meaning through choices, relationships, responsibility and care.
- `Tích đức` primarily changes Khanh's character, relationships and the human causal future. Whether there is metaphysical merit remains open.

## Author-side phenomenon schema

Every recurring or important supernatural phenomenon should track at least:

```yaml
observed: "What exactly occurred, without interpretation"
witnesses: []
physical_evidence: []
natural_models: []
folk_interpretations: []
supernatural_models: []
author_status: confirmed | strongly-implied | deliberately-ambiguous | unknown
repeatability: "whether/how it has recurred"
limits_observed: []
continuity_risk: []
```

### `limits_observed` rule

If a phenomenon has only been shown to appear in dreams and know one name, a future writer may **not** casually give it telekinesis, mind-reading, teleportation or direct-killing power.

This does not mean the author has proven the phenomenon incapable of those things. It means:

> **Absence of established ability is not permission to invent ability.**

Power or capability escalation requires explicit evidence and an author-level decision.

## Evidence discipline

For each scene/case, future agents should ask:

- What did Khanh directly perceive?
- What did another witness perceive?
- What exists independently of testimony?
- What is only memory?
- What is only dream?
- What is only a practitioner's interpretation?
- Which natural models remain viable?
- Which supernatural models remain viable?
- Does the author privately know the answer?
- If deliberately ambiguous, which models were designed in advance?

Never upgrade a character label into objective truth merely because that character is old, respected, religious, medically trained, technically skilled, or experienced.

**Authority may increase confidence; it never converts interpretation into objective truth.**
