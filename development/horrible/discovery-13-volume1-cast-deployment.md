# Discovery 13 — Volume 1 Cast Deployment & Relationship Arcs

Status: **accepted development decision**. Not yet Story Skills manuscript canon.

Purpose: control when recurring characters enter Volume 1, what ordinary-life reason gives them access to events, which embedded stories they can carry, and how relationships evolve without turning the ensemble into a fixed ghost-hunting team.

## Core deployment laws

- **Cast follows life topology, not plot convenience.**
- **A character only joins a case when their ordinary role gives them a reason to be there.**
- **Relationships progress even when mysteries do not.**
- **No one accompanies Khanh everywhere.**
- **Information remains socially distributed.**
- Absence is a valid pacing choice: not every recurring character needs a check-in during every arc.
- Do not form a standing `Khanh + Mai + practitioner + doctor + police + hacker` paranormal team.
- Do not use Bùi Thị Thủy as a supernatural hotline or Nguyễn Văn Nhân as a lore database.

## Volume 1 broad ensemble rhythm

### Phase 1 — Homecoming

Primary cast: Khanh, Lan, Hùng, Mai, then Hạnh.

Reader first learns the house, shop, market and family rhythm. Strange material enters only after ordinary life feels stable.

### Phase 2 — Case Zero

Khanh remains central. Lan and Mai support context through their real social/operational roles. Hùng and Hạnh provide moral and practical boundaries. No practitioner arrives to solve the case.

### Phase 3 — Aftermath / family memory

Grandfather notebook enters near Case Zero ending. Bùi Thị Thủy may be introduced in an ordinary cultural/commercial scene. Nguyễn Văn Nhân is seeded or introduced after the main Case Zero resolution, not as an emergency expert.

### Phase 4 — Return to Hanoi

Primary cast shifts to Nam, Hà, Đức Anh and light Keisuke contact. Home cast recedes naturally.

### Phase 5 — Case 02

Workplace cast dominates because the dead-account anomaly belongs to their ordinary work system. No hometown companion follows Khanh into the case.

### Phase 6 — Wider world

Phạm Quốc Duy may enter later through a medically plausible situation. Đỗ Minh Quân remains at the outer edge, preferably through an online retelling rather than immediate direct collaboration.

### Phase 7 — Volume 1 closing

Khanh notices that refusal notes such as `Không nhận`, `Không xem`, or `Không can thiệp` recur in grandfather's records across apparently unrelated matters. The ending opens a pattern rather than revealing a conspiracy.

## Opening deployment

### Chapter 1 cast

Keep the first chapter small: Khanh, mother Trần Thị Lan, father Nguyễn Văn Hùng, plus a brief natural appearance from Hoàng Ngọc Mai.

Lan appears before Hùng because she anchors the shop/home engine. Mai can pass through the market/shop ecosystem and tease Khanh with `Việt kiều Nhật` without receiving an exposition-heavy introduction.

Do not introduce Hạnh, Thủy, Nhân and the workplace cast in the opening.

The strange Case Zero customer should arrive around Chapter 2 or 3 rather than immediately. Chapter 1 should make the reader want to inhabit the home before asking them to fear it.

## Nguyễn Minh Hạnh and Lê Đức Tuấn

- Hạnh enters around Chapters 2–4, before Case Zero becomes serious, through a normal visit, phone call or practical request.
- She is not present when the strange order is first recorded; keep the direct witness/evidence circle small.
- She learns about the situation after the recipient dies and cautions Khanh against disturbing a grieving family merely because he is curious.
- Her Case Zero contribution is ethical/social perspective, not investigation fieldwork.
- Tuấn needs only a short early appearance to establish civil awkwardness with Khanh.
- Do not use Tuấn as a red herring merely because the relationship is distant.

## Hoàng Ngọc Mai in Case Zero

Mai is relatively visible in the first half of Case Zero, then recedes as the matter becomes a painful family secret rather than a curious local puzzle.

Her valid contributions come from operations/community knowledge:
- local routes and market rhythms;
- households and business connections;
- who normally moves through which area;
- practical introductions that keep Khanh from unrealistically barging into strangers' homes.

She initially finds the question interesting, then withdraws when she realizes Khanh has moved from identifying a customer into reopening a dead family's history.

Mai does not directly witness Case Zero's central supernatural residue. If Khanh thinks he sees the CCTV-like woman in the market, Mai does not confirm the sighting. She asks practical questions such as whether he actually saw the face clearly.

### Mai's embedded-story role

Volume 1 candidate: a 2–4-page-equivalent oral tale from a vegetable/food driver about seeing a dog behaving impossibly on an old road, possibly using a `chó đội nón mê`-type Northern oral-story variant if research supports the wording.

Mai frames it skeptically: the drivers tell stories, drink, exaggerate and drive at terrible hours. The tale is not investigated and is not evidence for the main case.

## Trần Thị Lan as Volume 1 oral storyteller

Lan is the strongest recurring embedded-story source in Volume 1.

Working rhythm: roughly 3–5 memorable stories across the Volume, not a quota.

The first should appear before Case Zero becomes frightening, establishing that strange customer stories are ordinary shop conversation. Begin with a gentle/uncanny story such as a dream of the dead, a memorial purchase or a recovered object rather than corpse horror.

Later stories can become darker independently of the main-case escalation.

At least one story may be something Khanh heard differently as a child, demonstrating oral mutation inside the family itself. Lan does not need to admit she was wrong; `Mẹ nhớ thế` is enough.

Voice principle:
> `Người ta kể thế, còn thật đến đâu ai biết.`

Use as a character tendency, not a repeated catchphrase every chapter.

## Nguyễn Văn Hùng

Hùng has low-volume, high-weight storytelling presence.

He generally refuses to linger on spiritual stories. When Khanh begins asking about grandfather, Hùng may eventually tell one short account that nobody else has told — ideally one where grandfather was wrong, failed to help, or could not determine what was happening.

This prevents the grandfather myth from becoming infallible.

Hùng does not know exactly what every refusal note means. A response such as `Ông mày viết nhiều kiểu lắm` is more appropriate than an answer dump.

Relationship movement: Khanh begins to understand that his father's avoidance is not simply disbelief; Hùng has learned that some questions cost more than they return.

## Nguyễn Văn Nhân

Do not bring Nhân into the main Case Zero investigation.

Introduce him after the main resolution, through ordinary family topology: a family visit, memorial, social visit, or Nhân visiting the house. Khanh may then use the opportunity to ask about grandfather.

First impression should be deliberately ordinary: tea, television, electricity prices, asking about Japan, family news.

Nhân does not decode `Không nhận lễ`. A stronger contribution is that he recognizes this as one of several kinds of refusal notes grandfather used, meaning the Case Zero note is not unique.

Nhân may carry one of Volume 1's strongest embedded oral stories. It need not be a clue.

## Bùi Thị Thủy

Thủy does not directly intervene in Case Zero.

Even if Lan suggests asking her, Khanh does not need to call her for diagnosis. This avoids teaching the reader that every uncanny event can be routed to a practitioner.

Introduce Thủy after Case Zero or during an unrelated cultural/commercial scene connected to votive/ritual business. Her first scene should be ordinary before spiritual: stock, fabric/clothes, money, transportation, preparation, family banter or electricity bills.

Do not introduce her by having her instantly detect an entity following Khanh.

She may tell a strange story matter-of-factly once the conversation naturally reaches it.

## Case Zero cast boundary

Case Zero should primarily belong to:
- Khanh — question-following center;
- Lan — shop memory / social context;
- Mai — local operational context, mainly first half;
- Hùng — boundary and family-memory pressure;
- Hạnh — moral/social boundary.

Thủy, Nhân, Duy, Quân, Long and the workplace cast do not become Case Zero investigation resources.

## Home-to-Hanoi interlude

Use approximately three chapter-equivalents after Case Zero before Case 02 becomes a real case:

1. **Departure / aftermath:** family routine, packing, notebook residue, perhaps a mild embedded story.
2. **Hanoi reset:** apartment, commute, office return, role adjustment. No required supernatural incident.
3. **Full work-life chapter:** team dynamics and ordinary engineering work; only near the end does a small anomaly appear, such as the deceased employee's username in a log.

The point is to make ordinary life genuinely resume before the next strange pattern forms.

## Workplace cast introduction

The office can naturally introduce several people in shared scenes, but each needs a distinct functional moment.

Preferred introduction order:
1. Trần Hoàng Nam — authority/work expectations;
2. Lê Thu Hà — client/project/stakeholder rhythm;
3. Vũ Đức Anh — junior/digital-folklore energy.

Keisuke appears lightly through Slack/Meet/email rather than requiring a major entrance.

## Case 02 deployment

### Vũ Đức Anh

Preferred first observer of the dead-account anomaly. He notices the username and reacts socially: `Account này của anh X chết rồi mà?`

Khanh initially interprets the problem as a credential/process failure.

Đức Anh participates because the production incident is his work, not because he joins a paranormal team. His fast online search and tendency to overfit can bring digital folklore into scenes, but Khanh does not allow social-media ghost theories into a formal incident report.

### Trần Hoàng Nam

Nam treats the first event as a security/operational problem and insists on proper reporting and scoped remediation.

Later tension: Khanh wants to continue digging after the actionable engineering problem is contained; Nam reminds him to fix what the team is responsible for first.

### Lê Thu Hà

Hà has moderate involvement through old project communication, stakeholder memory, client impact and source qualification. She does not become a ghost investigator.

### Workplace oral horror

At least one workplace story can circulate about the deceased employee appearing online after death or about another strange digital trace. At first it sounds like IT folklore. Its function is atmosphere/institutional memory rather than immediate proof.

## Sato Keisuke

Keisuke appears lightly in Volume 1 through normal work communication.

A single hint toward Khanh's Japan backstory is enough, for example Keisuke casually referencing a strange camera-related question Khanh once asked while onsite. Khanh changes the subject. Do not explain the Japan event immediately.

## Phạm Quốc Duy

Duy may appear in the later half of Volume 1 or a later short case, but not Case Zero.

Introduce him through a medically plausible context — accident, witness health issue, hospital contact, mutual connection — rather than creating a permanent skeptic role on demand.

His first-method impression is concrete: sleep, medication, intoxication, injury, neurological/physiological variables. He does not need to declare a philosophical position on ghosts.

Let him appear once and leave before becoming strongly recurring.

## Đỗ Minh Quân

Keep Quân near the outer edge until late Volume 1.

Preferred first presence: he publishes an exaggerated video/version of a local embedded story Khanh has already heard. Khanh notices that the online version differs materially from the oral one.

Quân does not initially know Khanh and does not immediately join him. Direct contact can wait until late Volume 1 or Volume 2.

This introduces modern folklore mutation without turning the series into a paranormal-content show.

## Đặng Việt Long

Do not force Long into Case Zero or Case 02 unless a genuine criminal/procedural threshold emerges.

His recurring role can begin in a later Volume 1 case or Volume 2. Police presence should follow jurisdiction and real need, not provide convenient records or authority.

## Embedded story distribution in Volume 1

Working target: roughly 6–10 memorable embedded tales of varying length, not a quota and not all mini-chapters.

Possible distribution:
- Lan: ~3;
- Mai / logistics network: 1–2;
- Nhân: 1;
- Thủy: 1;
- workplace: 1–2;
- Quân/internet: one mutated retelling/example.

At least one embedded tale should later prove heavily embellished so the reader learns the epistemology emotionally rather than only through explanation.

At least one embedded tale should remain strongly uncanny and plausibly supernatural without becoming a main case, so the oral layer does not become mere debunking texture.

## Relationship arcs across Volume 1

### Khanh ↔ Lan

Beginning: Khanh assumes Lan has heard so many strange stories that she has normalized them.

Movement: he begins respecting her practical ability to read people, motives and social context, even though this is not supernatural proof.

End: he asks her more questions, while Lan is not necessarily pleased that her son is becoming more interested. She can sincerely believe that knowing less is sometimes healthier.

### Khanh ↔ Hùng

Beginning: good relationship with limited emotional disclosure.

Case Zero friction: Hùng wants Khanh to stop once the matter no longer belongs to their family.

End: Khanh realizes his father may believe more than he says and that his refusal to ask further questions was learned through experience, not simple skepticism.

### Khanh ↔ Hạnh

Beginning: ordinary sibling banter and practical requests.

Movement: Hạnh explicitly challenges Khanh's assumption that discovering a truth gives him the right to expose it.

End: Khanh already withholds some things from Hạnh. This becomes an early rehearsal for the later Burden of Knowing arc.

### Khanh ↔ Mai

No Volume 1 romance arc.

Movement: childhood familiarity becomes adult mutual respect. Khanh recognizes the scale and competence of Mai's logistics/food operation role; Mai recognizes that the Japan-returned Khanh is steadier in some ways but more willing than before to keep pulling at unanswered questions.

No jealousy plot, third-party romantic bait or forced chemistry beats.

### Khanh ↔ workplace

Khanh returns with increased expectations after onsite work and has to re-enter team rhythms in Vietnam rather than behaving like an autonomous senior debugging alone.

Case 02 creates mild career friction rather than melodramatic damage: over-scoping, lost focus, or Nam reminding him that being correct is not the same as serving the team's current responsibility.

Hà may see the same trait from the stakeholder side: Khanh keeps searching for technical truth after the project already needs a decision.

## Information topology at Volume 1 end

No one holds the full picture.

- Lan knows much of Case Zero and some of Khanh's questions.
- Hùng knows more family/grandfather context than he reveals.
- Mai knows Case Zero from the local side but not Khanh's full later pattern.
- Hạnh knows a partial version.
- Nhân knows Khanh has started asking about grandfather and recognizes some record conventions.
- Workplace colleagues know the formal Case 02 incident but not necessarily Khanh's supernatural interpretation.
- Thủy has her own ritual/cultural knowledge but no master overview.

Do not create a group scene where all these information domains are pooled into one paranormal strategy meeting.

## Khanh's Volume 1 end state

Khanh has **not** become someone who actively seeks ghosts as a vocation.

Beginning model:
> a strange problem is something he wants to explain and close.

End model:
> some things do not disappear merely because he stops looking.

Near the Volume 1 ending, Khanh begins privately recording strange matters in a simple form:
- name;
- date;
- who told him;
- what seems certain;
- what remains uncertain.

He does not consciously imitate grandfather, but the resulting habit echoes the old notebook structure. This parallel should be visible to the reader without turning into chosen-heir symbolism.
