# Discovery 11 — Folklore Atlas & Embedded Ghost Story Engine

Status: **accepted development material, not yet Story Skills canon**.

## Purpose

`Horrible` should feel like Vietnamese spiritual / ghost fiction with mystery discipline, not detective fiction decorated with folklore. This layer gives the series a researched cultural memory and a flexible oral-horror engine.

The Atlas is **not a bestiary**. A named folk category is evidence of what people call or believe about a phenomenon, not automatic evidence that the author's universe contains a fixed species with reliable powers and weaknesses.

Core rules:

- **Folklore is evidence of what people believe, not evidence of what the universe is.**
- **A story told inside the novel is allowed to be more supernatural than the novel can prove.**
- **Real traditions deserve research; invented horror deserves clear fictional boundaries.**
- **Not every ghost story needs Khanh.**

## Cultural mission

The novel may help readers encounter the beauty, complexity and darker human edges of Vietnamese Buddhism, Đạo Mẫu / Thờ Mẫu, ancestor practice, folk belief, ritual culture, votive-paper commerce, funeral/memorial practice and oral supernatural lore.

"Celebrate" does not mean sanitizing living traditions. The story may show:

- devotion, music, color, art, memory, care and community;
- grief rituals and ways the living remain connected to the dead;
- practitioners who genuinely help or comfort people;
- commercialization, fraud, fear, social pressure and exploitation;
- sincere believers who are still wrong;
- disagreements between practitioners, families, Buddhist voices and folk interpretations.

Negative behavior belongs to specific people, incentives and situations. It must not be used as proof that an entire living tradition is sinister.

## Atlas architecture

Each folklore entry should eventually track:

```text
TERM
REGION / COMMUNITY
SOURCE QUALITY
RELIGIOUS / FOLK / ORAL / MODERN-MEDIA LAYER
EARLIEST OR STRONGEST SOURCES
COMMON DESCRIPTION
KNOWN VARIANTS
WHAT IT IS OFTEN CONFUSED WITH
REAL CULTURAL CONTEXT
FICTIONAL USES ALLOWED
SENSITIVITY / MISREPRESENTATION RISK
CHARACTERS WHO MIGHT BELIEVE IT
AUTHOR-UNIVERSE STATUS
EMBEDDED-STORY CANDIDATES
```

Do not assume one term describes one objective supernatural species. The same label may cover different stories, and the same phenomenon may receive different labels from different communities or authorities.

Narration does not certify which local name is metaphysically correct.

## Source discipline

Treat sources by role rather than collapsing them:

1. primary/community/institutional documentation;
2. academic research;
3. reputable journalism;
4. oral testimony;
5. internet folklore, entertainment and viral retellings.

Lower-confidence sources are still valuable for understanding **how a story is told today**, but they do not automatically prove historical continuity or religious doctrine.

Hard rule:

> Do not write `người Việt xưa tin rằng...` from a viral modern concept without adequate historical/cultural sourcing.

If a concept is compelling but poorly sourced:

- put it in a character's mouth as hearsay;
- make it explicitly local fictional lore; or
- fictionalize it without falsely attaching it to a real religion/tradition.

## Embedded ghost-story engine

Stories told by other people are a major horror channel independent of Khanh's investigated cases.

Possible delivery forms:

- customers talking in the votive-paper shop;
- family meals and memorial gatherings;
- old people retelling local incidents;
- logistics drivers talking on night routes;
- voice messages and calls;
- coworkers and office urban legends;
- online threads / livestream / content-creator retellings;
- ritual practitioners discussing previous work;
- a Buddhist monastic or layperson recounting a grieving family's experience;
- grandfather's surviving half-brother telling his own version of past events.

A story may be:

- accurate;
- exaggerated;
- misremembered;
- stitched from two separate incidents;
- hearsay repeated as certainty;
- intentionally embellished for effect;
- motivated by reputation, shame or fear;
- genuinely supernatural;
- impossible to classify.

A told story is testimony. It is not author-canon merely because the narration temporarily reconstructs the teller's version vividly.

### Reconstructed oral scenes

The prose may temporarily enter a vivid reconstructed scene rather than printing pages of quoted dialogue. Reader orientation must remain clear that this is **the teller's version**, not omniscient historical fact.

Different tellers should sound different. The same incident may be told multiple times with meaningful contradictions.

### Payoff distribution

Most embedded tales do **not** need plot payoff.

Functions may include:

- pure scare / entertainment;
- cultural texture;
- worldview;
- character revelation;
- comic social storytelling;
- grief and remembrance;
- thematic echo;
- false pattern;
- distant foreshadowing;
- occasional bridge into a later case.

Only when a claim crosses into the main plot must it be re-evaluated through the Discovery 09 evidence framework:

`observed → witnesses → physical evidence → natural models → folk interpretations → supernatural models → author status`.

## Recurring storyteller ecosystems

### Mother

Decades in the shop give her a large archive of customer stories. She is not an omniscient collector and often filters stories pragmatically: `Nghe thì nghe thế thôi.`

### Grandfather's younger paternal half-brother

A major source for old spiritual-working culture and contradictory memory of grandfather. He may have his own framework and may disagree with grandfather's choices. He must not become an answer database.

### Hometown logistics friend

Her world naturally generates modern night-route folklore: drivers, markets, depots, warehouses, accidents, cold-storage spaces, roads before dawn and rumors moving through supply networks.

### Recurring ritual / medium acquaintance

Her stories inherit her vocabulary and worldview. Her terminology is expert testimony within a tradition, not author voice.

### Buddhist characters

They may contribute spiritual, ethical and grief-centered interpretations and may explicitly refuse to label an experience as a ghost. Monastics are not an automatic anti-ghost/exorcist class.

### Workplace network

Modern urban folklore: offices, server rooms, apartments, hospitals, former employees, access logs, night shifts and Japanese workplace stories later in the series.

### Internet / content creators

Useful for showing folklore mutation under algorithms, click incentives, simplification and retelling. A viral version may diverge drastically from the originating event.

## Initial Atlas clusters

These are accepted research / story directions, not confirmed species canon.

### Human-dead vocabulary

- `hồn`
- `vong`
- `vong hồn`
- `âm hồn`
- `oan hồn`
- `cô hồn`

Treat these first as cultural / conversational categories. Usage may overlap but need not be technically identical.

`oan hồn` does not mean morally righteous or omniscient. A victim may also have been harmful, biased or deceptive.

`cô hồn` requires careful research because folk practice, ritual context and colloquial usage overlap.

### Buddhist-linked concepts

`ngạ quỷ` requires explicit separation from generic colloquial `ma đói` or `cô hồn`. If used in a Buddhist explanatory context, research doctrine and local practice rather than importing horror-media definitions.

Buddhist concepts such as nghiệp, luân hồi or ngạ quỷ may appear through characters and traditions without becoming narrator-certified cosmology.

### Dreams and return of relatives

Stories of deceased relatives appearing in dreams are a strong recurring motif. They can be tender, frightening or ambiguous and do not require investigation.

### `ma trơi`

A strong epistemology example because the folk label and natural explanations can coexist in reader awareness. Useful around fields, cemeteries and dark rural edges without forcing a supernatural verdict.

### `bóng đè`

Suitable for oral stories and Khanh's Japan backstory while preserving serious physiological / sleep explanations.

### `vong nhập` / `áp vong` / `nhập`

Track terminology and provenance separately. Different religious, folk and medical voices may disagree. Do not merge them into one programmable possession mechanic.

### `trùng tang`

Strong case / oral-story candidate. Keep explicit distinction between folk belief, family fear, contemporary interpretation and any Buddhist/academic criticism or reinterpretation. The fear of trùng can itself create powerful human consequences even without author-confirmed supernatural causation.

### `Thần Trùng`

Research-required. Do not use as main-plot fact until its historical, temple, folk and modern-media layers have been separated.

### `quỷ nhập tràng`

Accepted as a rare major-horror candidate. Keep restrained and physically disturbing rather than zombie/action spectacle. Track folk retellings, corpse-handling beliefs and plausible postmortem explanations separately.

### `chó đội nón mê`

Strong Northern oral-story candidate. Prefer first use as a driver's or local person's story rather than confirmed monster canon.

### `tinh` / `yêu` / `quỷ`

High-research umbrella terms. Do not manufacture a fixed taxonomy from casual modern usage.

### Region-sensitive terms

Do not import a famous Vietnamese ghost term into Northern-core folklore merely because it is nationally recognizable.

Examples requiring regional caution:

- `ma da` — do not default it into Northern folklore;
- `ma xó` — variants / provenance need research;
- `ma giữ của` — modern-media and folk layers need separation.

## Đạo Mẫu / Thờ Mẫu boundary

Hầu đồng is not default horror possession.

It may feel powerful, uncanny, beautiful, overwhelming or difficult for Khanh to interpret without the novel reframing a living practice as demonic mechanics.

Cases may involve:

- a thanh đồng's statement;
- a detail apparently impossible for them to know;
- money or commercialization;
- family conflict;
- mistaken interpretation;
- fraud;
- sincere practice;
- emotional transformation.

Do not write `Đạo Mẫu secretly summons demons` as a default plot premise.

## Buddhist boundary

Buddhism is not an anti-ghost magic system, and monks/nuns are not automatic exorcists.

A spiritually mature character may say `Tôi không biết chuyện anh thấy là gì.` This epistemic humility is valuable to the series.

## Initial Volume 1 mini-story bank

These are accepted **candidate seeds**, not plot canon.

### 1. Chuyện xe rau lúc ba giờ sáng

A driver connected to the hometown friend's family claims he saw a dog moving upright on an old road before dawn. Others immediately challenge alcohol, fatigue and distance. No investigation required.

### 2. Cuộc gọi sau khi chết

A shop customer says a dead relative's phone once called the family. The story is technically explainable but gains different resonance after `Tài khoản của người chết`.

### 3. Hai chị em cùng mơ

Two relatives report overlapping dreams of the same deceased person, with only a few specific shared details rather than identical dream scripts.

### 4. Đèn ngoài nghĩa địa

An older person calls distant lights `ma trơi`; someone younger offers a natural explanation. Neither version needs to win.

### 5. Nhà có tang sợ trùng

The horror may be the family's escalating fear, coercion and damage to relationships as much as any supernatural question.

### 6. Một giá đồng nói sai

A teller reports that a thanh đồng said several obviously wrong things, then one deeply specific detail that no one can explain. No investigation required.

### 7. Ông nội từng từ chối một nhà

A local person retells one of grandfather's refusals as folklore. Much later, the remembered version can conflict with grandfather's actual notebook entry.

## Story bridge system

An embedded tale may reappear volumes later, but reuse should be planned rather than retrofitted casually.

First appearance should normally remain unhighlighted. A later return can create the reread reaction: `Khoan, chuyện này mình từng nghe rồi.`

Both directions are allowed:

- a throwaway folk story later proves to derive from a real event;
- a seemingly relevant folk story later proves to be a mutated rumor and false pattern.

## Glossary policy

Do not footnote cultural terms in the middle of horror prose by default.

Explain through context and natural character interaction. An end-of-Volume cultural glossary may be considered later, but it must explain terminology/cultural context rather than declare supernatural truth.

## Relationship to main supernatural rules

Folklore terminology is allowed to be richer, stranger and more contradictory than the author's confirmed universe.

An oral story may contain powers or rules never observed in main narrative. Future writers must **not** copy those powers into reality merely because they appeared in a tale.

Discovery 09 remains authoritative for reality-layer capability continuity.
