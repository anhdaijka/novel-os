# Horrible — Consolidated Discovery 01–06

Status: **accepted development material**.

## 1. Protagonist — Khanh

- Male, 27.
- Software engineer; career deliberately mirrors the author's real technical background, but the novel remains fiction.
- Recently returned to Vietnam after several years of onsite work in Japan.
- The onsite period ended normally; no scandal, firing or career collapse.
- He will resume work at the Hanoi branch of a Vietnam–Japan technology company after spending a few weeks at home.
- He is a skeptical believer: unwilling to claim the supernatural does not exist, but equally unwilling to treat dreams, testimony or ritual interpretation as automatic fact.
- He still experiences bodily fear around darkness, uncanny spaces and death, though he is no longer childishly incapacitated by ghost stories.
- He has no confirmed supernatural ability.
- His engineering instinct makes him seek causal chains, contradictions, reproducibility and root causes.
- His deeper flaw is that he struggles to tolerate questions that may never receive a clean explanation.
- He is not initially driven by heroic altruism. Curiosity, discomfort with inconsistencies and specific personal stakes pull him into cases.

### Japan backstory

During his years in Japan, Khanh experienced some episodes he could not confidently classify: nightmares, stress, coincidence, psychological projection or genuinely local supernatural phenomena.

On several occasions he called his parents in Vietnam. Despite being people familiar with spiritual stories and practices, they tended to calm him down and deny the frightening interpretation rather than encourage it. The story does not initially settle whether they truly believed nothing was wrong or deliberately avoided making a son alone abroad more afraid.

Japan should remain background pressure early. Do not open the novel with a large Japan haunting sequence. Small memories, sensory triggers, dreams or later business travel may gradually reactivate that history.

## 2. Khanh's long character arc

Khanh initially tends to believe that if a truth can be discovered, then revealing it is usually the intellectually honest thing to do.

A later major case should challenge that assumption. He learns a horrifying truth concerning a family, but judges that disclosure would mainly traumatize innocent descendants and cannot repair the original harm. He chooses silence.

Later, one descendant / relative from that family enters Khanh's ordinary professional or social world. Repeated contact makes the secret increasingly corrosive. The person may trust and value Khanh without knowing what he carries.

The series should not pre-decide that confession is automatically morally superior. The enduring question is: **does knowing the truth create an obligation to disclose it?**

Khanh's broader ethical development moves from practical cynicism about “ăn cơm nhà vác tù và hàng tổng” toward becoming a person more willing to help when he genuinely can. He continues doing his normal work well; he does not need to become a spiritual professional. “Tích đức” becomes a chosen way of living, not a guaranteed supernatural reward mechanic.

## 3. Family and home base

### Votive-paper shop

Khanh's family has operated a votive-paper / ritual-goods business for decades. The family home and shop share the same building on a main road immediately beside a market crossroads.

The area is especially dense on full-moon days, first lunar days, festivals, ritual seasons and busy market periods. Customers, motorbikes, voices, incense, paper goods, deliveries and neighborhood traffic should make the home base feel vivid and lived in.

The shop is a natural story intake node, not a magical quest board. Customers may tell stories, order unusual items, mention deaths, rituals, family disputes or strange experiences. However, many later cases should originate elsewhere.

### Mother

- Primarily manages the shop.
- Open to spiritual possibility but practical rather than gullible.
- Decades of retail and ritual-related customers make her unusually good at reading people.
- She can often distinguish someone who is genuinely frightened from someone bragging, performing, exaggerating or simply needing to talk.
- Her intuitions about people may be strong while her explanations for causes may still be wrong.
- She is a social-memory node, not a permanent investigative partner.

### Father

- Practical, quieter, likely connected to construction / engineering / business rather than spiritual practice.
- Does not need to reject spirituality, but is less conversational about it.
- Relationship with Khanh is fundamentally good but emotionally understated.
- He knows much more about grandfather's past than Khanh does, yet much of that knowledge feels to him like old adult business rather than lore his son was entitled to receive.
- He may intentionally avoid some subjects because he thinks “biết để làm gì?”, not because of a giant conspiracy.

### Older sister

- Roughly 7–8 years older than Khanh.
- Primary-school teacher.
- Married and living in a neighboring district.
- Loves Khanh but is practical rather than endlessly indulgent.
- If she needs an item or errand and thinks of her younger brother, she may ask him; this is not a relationship where Khanh asks and she automatically provides.
- She can tease him about childhood fears, but not cruelly.
- Khanh and her husband are civil but cold / distant. There is no necessary feud or hidden betrayal; they simply find each other difficult to become close to.

## 4. Grandfather and family memory

Khanh's deceased paternal grandfather was once very well known in the region. Different people may describe his work differently: thầy bói, thầy cúng, xem đất, giải hạn, “biết việc âm”, or simply “đi xem thầy”. The novel should not rush to define a single clean professional label.

The grandfather may have helped people, failed people, made mistakes and kept things private. Do not idealize him into a saint or twist him into a secret villain for shock value.

He did not leave a magical manual. What remains should feel like practical records: names, dates, lunar dates, villages, addresses, terse notes, marks, calendars and fragments that Khanh does not initially understand.

Grandfather is a gateway into past local memory, not the answer to all supernatural events.

### Surviving paternal half-brother

One younger half-brother of grandfather is still alive. They share the same father; their father once spent a long period working away from home and had a second wife and child there.

The two branches later recognized the relationship and maintain warm, regular family contact.

This surviving half-brother also did some work in a spiritual / ritual sphere related to grandfather's world. He can therefore be a valuable memory witness and interpretive counterpoint.

He must not become an all-knowing lore database. His version of grandfather may differ materially from what Khanh's father, customers, old villagers or other relatives remember.

## 5. Hometown female friend

- Female, close enough in age to Khanh to share local history.
- Works in logistics near the market crossroads.
- Her family is one of the commune's largest suppliers of vegetables and food.
- Through trade, transport, suppliers, shopkeepers and market networks, she naturally hears a great deal of local information.
- She is lively enough to be curious about strange stories but not obligated to accompany Khanh into danger.
- She is not default romance and not a permanent Watson.
- Familiar local teasing may call Khanh “Việt kiều Nhật”. Do not use “Cán bộ Nhật”.

## 6. Supporting network principles

The cast is a social graph, not a ghost-hunting party.

Useful recurring viewpoints include:

- a spiritual practitioner / đồng-type family acquaintance: experienced, ordinary in daily life, neither infallible nor fake by default;
- a recurring skeptic, potentially medical / legal / investigative professional, who insists that “not explained” is not equivalent to “ghost”;
- later younger believers / content creators who know folklore but may exaggerate because narrative attention has incentives;
- law-enforcement characters introduced only when organically relevant, never as convenient private-data suppliers.

Every recurring character should continue to have work, family and concerns outside Khanh's cases.

## 7. Setting

Contemporary Northern Vietnam.

The hometown may be fictionalized while using real higher-level geographical and cultural reference points. The accepted texture leans toward the Kinh Bắc / former Bắc Ninh–Bắc Giang sphere, including real commercial flows in ritual goods and the practical closeness to Hanoi.

The hometown should plausibly sit within 1–2 hours of Hanoi so Khanh can return on weekends without living there full time.

Prefer a transition area rich enough to support town, old villages, market, fields, river, low hills, roads, industrial expansion, cemeteries, temples, workshops and new development.

The setting must generate story rather than act as Vietnamese decoration pasted onto generic horror.

## 8. Series engine

Cases can arrive through:

- the family shop and customer stories;
- family and extended-family networks;
- Khanh's hometown friend and market logistics network;
- software work in Hanoi;
- colleagues, clients and Japanese-side contacts;
- ordinary news, social media, public records and local rumor;
- connections Khanh notices only after an unrelated experience gives him a new analogy.

No single delivery mechanism should dominate forever.

## 9. Case-resolution philosophy

Allowed outcomes:

- solved;
- mostly solved;
- partially solved;
- unresolved;
- dormant and reopened much later;
- intentionally ontologically ambiguous;
- wrong closure, where Khanh's previous explanation later proves incomplete or false.

A case that remains unresolved for Khanh should still have deliberately designed causal models behind it. Do not hide author uncertainty behind atmosphere.

Khanh may fail, miss clues, trust the wrong person or arrive at a theory that is only half right. Professional investigators should remain plausible professionals.

## 10. Supernatural epistemology

- The supernatural may genuinely exist.
- The novel should avoid defining too much too early.
- The dead retain limitations of knowledge, memory, motive and personality.
- A dead person may love, resent, misremember, manipulate, omit or lie.
- An oan hồn may genuinely need help while still using a living person for a self-interested goal.
- A dying person may hide something just as the living do.
- Apparitions and possession-like phenomena can be powerful without establishing a clean bestiary.
- “Quỷ nhập tràng” and related horror motifs are available as ambiguous cases, not automatic proof of a non-human demon taxonomy.
- Strong experiences can be experienced as real by Khanh without the author providing final metaphysical certification.
- Not every ghost appearance is an information delivery event.

## 11. Novel-wide language balance

The novel must never drift into “detective fiction with supernatural decoration”.

Preferred overall feel, not a mechanical quota:

- horror / spiritual / uncanny atmosphere is the largest component;
- everyday life, relationships, work and social drama occupy substantial space;
- mystery / investigation is important but secondary;
- physical confrontation is rare.

Investigation scenes should still feel like horror when appropriate: awkward homes, withheld names, ritual objects, old photographs, social silence, memory gaps, mundane rooms that become disturbing because of what is learned there.

**Mystery must sharpen the horror, never sterilize it.**

**Solving the mechanism does not necessarily solve the haunting.**

## 12. Discovery 02 — Case Zero shell

Working concept: **Đơn hàng cho người chưa chết**.

- Khanh is already home about three days when the novel opens.
- Opening is ordinary: waking late, market noise, family shop activity, work notifications he is trying not to engage with during his break.
- The Japan backstory appears only as tiny residue, not a prologue haunting.
- Near a busy ritual / market day, Khanh helps his mother write an order because the shop is crowded.
- A normal-looking woman in her 50s–60s orders highly specific votive items for a man who is still alive.
- She names the recipient and gives enough location information for Khanh's mother to recognize the family.
- She pays cash and plans to return in three days.
- Four days later the man dies.
- The dead man's family comes to the shop to buy funeral goods and knows nothing about the earlier order.
- Khanh initially investigates only the identity of the purchaser, partly because such an order could create harmful rumors around his family's business.
- Father and sister initially advise him not to get involved in another family's grief.

## 13. Discovery 03 — Case Zero accepted truth structure

### Present-day deceased man

- Male, roughly late 50s / early 60s.
- Financially comfortable, respectable enough locally, not a cartoon elite villain.
- Previously worked in practical accounting / management / local enterprise and later ran smaller business activity.
- He lived many ordinary years and may be a decent father / grandfather while still carrying one serious failure from the past.

### Present death

- He dies after falling down stairs at home at night, suffering fatal head injury.
- Alcohol, blood pressure, darkness, poor sleep and ordinary household conditions make accidental death plausible.
- No required physical evidence of assault.
- The key mystery is not necessarily “who killed him?” but “why was he moving through the house in that state at that hour?”

### Strange order

The purchaser asks for a votive object made to resemble an old radio cassette / similarly specific personal object. The radio-cassette direction is preferred because it can carry memory and sound motifs without becoming magical technology.

The order includes recipient name and lunar-age information. The purchaser name is also written into the shop's order record but is not highlighted to the reader initially.

### Surviving sister

The woman on CCTV strongly resembles the dead man's surviving younger sister, but the sister denies coming to the shop and appears genuinely confused / offended.

CCTV is ordinary shop footage, useful but not forensic certainty.

A minor clue: the purchaser uses her left hand while the surviving sister is right-handed.

### Buried family history

Roughly thirty years earlier, the family had a third sibling — a young woman whose death became a subject the older generation gradually stopped discussing.

The present-day dead man did not necessarily kill her. His moral failure is that he knew important truth about an accident / preventable death and chose silence when he had an opportunity to speak.

The surviving sister knows only part of what happened, and three decades of memory may have changed even that.

A family photograph eventually reveals the forgotten third sibling.

### Human pressure before the new death

A living person connected to the old tragedy deliberately sends the present victim an old image / message / object meant to reopen the past and pressure him toward confession or acknowledgment.

The sender does not intend to kill him.

The reminder contributes to stress, insomnia and behavior preceding the stair fall.

Khanh eventually identifies this sender, producing a strong human causal model.

### False solution

Khanh initially collapses several actions into one clean theory: the person applying psychological pressure must also be the woman who placed the votive order.

That root-cause model later breaks. The living sender can be shown with strong evidence not to be the shop purchaser.

Thus the human layer largely resolves while the purchaser identity remains open.

### Purchaser models retained

H — human impersonation by someone who knew the old family story.

S — supernatural appearance of the woman who died around thirty years earlier.

P — the surviving sister did come to the shop under a psychological / memory / identity condition and genuinely does not remember or cannot acknowledge it.

H and S should remain the strongest early models; P remains possible but weaker.

Do not later retcon Case Zero evidence casually.

### Residue

The name written as “customer” on the original order turns out to be the name of the woman who died thirty years earlier.

The purchaser also knew unusually precise details of the old radio cassette, though at least one living person may still provide a human route for that knowledge.

Later comparison of old imagery may suggest the CCTV woman looks more like the dead third sibling than the surviving sister, while resemblance, image quality and confirmation bias preserve ambiguity.

### Grandfather tag

Only after the human case has mostly settled does Khanh encounter an old grandfather record while looking for something else.

It contains the dead woman's name, an old date / village and a terse phrase such as:

> Không nhận lễ.

Khanh does not understand what the note means.

Grandfather did not solve Case Zero for him and is not used as a supernatural detective shortcut.

## 14. Discovery 05 — Case Zero beat architecture

Accepted sequence:

1. Khanh's third day home: ordinary market / family rhythm.
2. Hometown friend and local life establish “Việt kiều Nhật” teasing and social normality.
3. Khanh helps mother record the unusual order during a crowded period.
4. Only after the customer leaves does mother notice the named recipient is alive.
5. Khanh dismisses it as mistake / superstition / preparation for illness.
6. Four days later the recipient dies.
7. His family visits the shop for ordinary funeral purchases and knows nothing of the old order.
8. Khanh's first goal is simply to identify the purchaser and protect his family's shop from harmful rumor.
9. CCTV points toward the surviving sister.
10. The surviving sister denies visiting, and Khanh realizes his questions are imposing on a grieving family.
11. The left-hand detail and the extremely specific radio cassette deepen the mismatch.
12. Khanh sees a woman resembling the CCTV purchaser in a busy market, follows and loses her. No one verifies the sighting.
13. Old photos and testimony reveal a third sibling who died about thirty years earlier.
14. The purchaser name on the original order becomes newly meaningful.
15. Evidence reveals someone living has been pressuring the present victim with the old secret.
16. Khanh builds a clean human theory.
17. That theory partially collapses when the living sender cannot plausibly be the purchaser.
18. Present death remains largely compatible with accident.
19. Old family wrong is partially acknowledged / handled without turning Khanh into a public exposé hero.
20. Purchaser remains unidentified.
21. Near the end, Khanh finds grandfather's terse old note.
22. Khanh still returns to Hanoi to resume normal work rather than deciding to become a supernatural investigator.

Expected Case Zero length: roughly 12–16 chapters once later mapped.

## 15. Discovery 06 — Work life

Khanh returns to a Vietnam–Japan technology company in Hanoi.

- Around five years with the company, with a substantial recent onsite period in Japan.
- Senior-ish full-stack engineer.
- Still writes code and handles technical incidents.
- Can communicate directly with Japanese-side colleagues.
- Not yet formal management.
- Career question: remain deeply technical or move toward BrSE / lead work.
- Strong at reading code, dependencies and production failures.
- Weakness: may spend too long understanding an anomaly beyond the immediate scope of the fix.

He lives alone in a normal small apartment / studio in Hanoi and usually commutes by motorbike. The apartment is not automatically haunted.

### Workplace cast

Team Lead:
- male, about 34–36;
- competent, pragmatic, dry rather than toxic;
- respects Khanh but warns him against unnecessary scope expansion;
- mostly indifferent to supernatural debates.

Junior Engineer:
- male, about 23–24;
- fast, smart, online, meme / creepypasta aware, slightly chaotic;
- useful for quick discovery but prone to weak sources and pattern overfitting.

BA / BrSE colleague:
- female, about Khanh's age;
- organized, socially perceptive, focused on what the client actually needs;
- may clash with Khanh when he prioritizes technical correctness over product context;
- not designated as romance.

Japanese-side counterpart:
- recurring professional contact from the onsite period;
- appears through work calls / messages and occasional travel;
- not immediately turned into a supernatural bridge back to Japan.

### Work texture

Use credible software life: logs, production incidents, database behavior, API contracts, race conditions, cache, deployment, handover and Japanese-client communication. Keep detail believable but subordinate to story.

Technical experience may give Khanh analogies for investigation — for example, two witnesses agreeing may simply mean they share the same bad source — but never magical deduction.

## 16. Case 02 accepted candidate — Tài khoản của người chết

Case Zero deliberately gives way to 2–4 meaningful chapters of ordinary re-entry into Hanoi life before the next case fully forms.

A deceased engineer's account / credential appears in a production incident.

- The engineer died in an apparently ordinary traffic accident months earlier.
- The credential should have been disabled.
- Initial activity is mundane configuration / system behavior, not a ghostly message.
- Security handling remains professional; Khanh does not cowboy-investigate a corporate breach alone.
- Human misuse / shared credential / process failure explains most of the incident.
- A living person may be trying to retrieve, hide or restore data the dead engineer left behind: logs, recordings, notes, complaint evidence or material around workplace wrongdoing.
- The prior death need not secretly become murder.
- A small behavior in the account activity resembles the deceased engineer's personal technical habit, though imitation is possible.
- After the human misuse is substantially resolved, one later access event occurs in a context that is hard to reproduce or attribute, perhaps after rotation / revocation or in a system the impersonator did not know.
- Possible technical explanations remain: delayed automation, timestamp problems, cache, undocumented systems or stale credentials.
- The uncanny activity should do something restrained, such as opening a familiar file or dashboard, not send “I WAS MURDERED”.

Case 02 has **no direct Case Zero / grandfather conspiracy connection**.

The thematic rhyme is enough:

- Case Zero: family memory, handwritten orders and traces of the dead in local social systems.
- Case 02: accounts, logs and traces of the dead in digital institutional systems.

Khanh is the only obvious common denominator at this stage, forcing the question: are strange events actually clustering around him, or is he simply paying attention now?

## 17. Current next discovery

Discovery 07 should design long-series architecture and macro mystery while preserving horror-first language and preventing escalation into power fantasy, demon-boss mythology or a single conspiracy behind every case.
