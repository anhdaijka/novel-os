# Discovery 14 — Volume 1 Architecture & Case Map

Status: **accepted development decision**. Not yet Story Skills manuscript canon.

## Core Volume contract

Volume 1 is not `Case Zero + Case 02 + another investigation`.

Accepted structure:

- two full mystery/investigation cases;
- one shorter spiritual haunting arc that is **not** treated as a case in manuscript logic;
- ordinary-life, cultural and embedded-folklore chapters between and around them.

Core laws:

- **Two mysteries are enough. The third supernatural arc does not need to become an investigation.**
- **The Volume must contain horror even while no case is active.**
- **A macro pattern may become visible without revealing a macro cause.**
- **Volume 1 ends with Khanh beginning to record uncertainty, not beginning a career as a ghost investigator.**

## Working size

- Target range: roughly 44–48 chapters.
- Current working map: 46 chapters.
- Approximate word-count envelope: 120k–145k Vietnamese words, not a quota.
- Chapter counts remain movable during scene outlining and drafting.

## Seven-phase architecture

### Phase I — `Trở về` — Chapters 1–3

Purpose: establish home, shop, market, family, ordinary warmth and oral-story culture before serious horror.

- Ch.1: Khanh / Lan / Hùng; Mai appears lightly; no main horror event.
- Ch.2: family/shop life; Hạnh may enter; strange woman places the unusual votive order near the end or latter half.
- Ch.3: specificity of order becomes uncomfortable; Lan thinks the named recipient may still be alive; Khanh rationalizes.
- Japan backstory remains a faint memory/hint, not an opening horror scene.

### Phase II — `Đơn hàng cho người chưa chết` — roughly Chapters 4–16

Case Zero remains the larger opening case.

Working rhythm:
- death around Ch.5–6;
- first question is only `who placed the order?`;
- Ch.2–8: order, death, funeral-goods return, CCTV, living sister denial, Mai/local context;
- Ch.9–10: third sibling dead for about thirty years becomes visible; old-photo/family-history layer changes the case;
- Ch.10–14: buried history, reminder campaign by a living person, victim's silence, false clean explanation;
- living sender proves they did not place the votive order;
- Ch.15: human/family layer mostly closes; current death remains plausibly accidental;
- Ch.16: purchaser identity stays unresolved; grandfather notebook reveals the dead woman's name and terse `Không nhận lễ`-type residue.

No arrest climax and no metaphysical closure.

Case Zero includes roughly 2–3 memorable embedded stories. None should mirror the exact case too neatly.

### Phase III — `Những ngày tưởng đã xong` — Chapters 17–19

Purpose: genuine aftermath and life resumption.

- family routine and Khanh preparing to return to Hanoi;
- Thủy may receive an ordinary-life introduction, not supernatural diagnosis;
- Nhân may appear through normal family topology after Case Zero is effectively over;
- Nhân may reveal only that grandfather used refusal notes more than once, not what they mean;
- Khanh does not yet catalog the notebooks;
- Ch.19 moves him back toward Hanoi/apartment/work life.

### Phase IV — `Hà Nội` — Chapters 20–21

Purpose: prove Khanh has a real second life.

- office return;
- Nam, Hà and Đức Anh establish normal work dynamics;
- one mostly ordinary engineering/work chapter;
- Keisuke may lightly hint at an old Japan oddity;
- dead-account anomaly appears only near the end of Ch.21 or start of Ch.22.

### Phase V — `Tài khoản của người chết` — Chapters 22–33

Case 02 is shorter than Case Zero and remains rooted in modern work systems.

Working rhythm:
- Ch.22–24: mundane credential/process explanations remain strongest;
- Ch.25–28: dead engineer's preserved notes/data/complaint/evidence and human misconduct emerge;
- living person uses dead account to hide or restore something;
- Ch.30–31: human misuse largely solved;
- Ch.32: after revoke/rotate/containment, one separate-system activity remains unexplained and outside the known impersonator's awareness;
- Ch.33: aftermath; Khanh cannot reduce the residue to certainty but also does not claim a ghost used a computer.

No full apparition is required in Case 02. Horror comes from identity-like traces persisting in systems designed to record human agency.

Technical detail is included only when it narrows a human model, deepens the uncanny identity problem or advances character/work tension.

### Phase VI — `Một chuyện không cần giải` — Chapters 34–40

This is **not Case 03** in manuscript logic.

Working short-haunting shell: **`Người về ăn giỗ`**.

Purpose: correct genre balance after two mystery-heavy arcs and demonstrate that helping does not require proving the supernatural.

Accepted shell:
- Khanh is home or drawn into the event through ordinary family/customer topology, possibly accompanying Lan or helping deliver ritual/votive goods around a death anniversary;
- family involved is ordinary and has no unresolved murder that needs solving;
- longstanding small anniversary phenomena may include dreams, displaced objects, a chair/door/sound, remembered presence or other low-level uncanny details;
- during Khanh's visit, he experiences one strongly uncanny, near-full apparition that looks like an ordinary person rather than a grotesque ghost;
- Khanh later describes a detail or habit connected to the deceased that he had no obvious reason to know;
- no practitioner arrives to certify what happened;
- emotional core involves a very ordinary unfinished promise, object, message or relationship duty rather than hidden crime;
- Khanh helps because the act is worth doing even if the apparition was misperceived;
- whether the haunting stops after the promise is addressed remains unproven;
- the arc ends with residue, not an explanation.

Possible grandfather overlap is extremely light: later notebook material may contain the same locality/family name with a refusal such as `Không xem`, but no proof that grandfather knew this exact haunting.

Exact deceased person, family, apparition mechanics, promise/object, ritual detail and overlap remain deliberately unlocked for Discovery 15.

### Phase VII — `Những việc ông từng từ chối` — Chapters 41–46

Purpose: close Volume 1 on pattern recognition, not conspiracy revelation.

- ordinary life continues;
- Quân may first enter only through an online, exaggerated mutation of a story Khanh already heard;
- Duy may appear only if naturally useful; otherwise defer him to Volume 2;
- Khanh reopens grandfather's notebooks because of an ordinary trigger, not a vision or supernatural command;
- he notices repeated refusal language across years: `Không nhận`, `Không xem`, `Thôi`, `Để nhà tự giải`, `Không can thiệp`, or similar terse phrasing;
- there are roughly a dozen-plus refusal entries across multiple notebooks, enough to feel intentional but not like a giant occult database;
- entries span different ordinary categories: people, land, mourning, illness, dreams, disappearances, family disputes and other matters;
- Case Zero appears among this material;
- Case 02 does **not** appear and must not be retroactively made a grandfather prophecy;
- the short haunting may have only a loose locality/name resemblance at most.

The final revelation is simply:

> Grandfather encountered enough matters that he had an established practice of explicitly refusing some of them, and Khanh does not know why.

Macro question opened:

> Why would a man remembered for receiving people, reading situations and helping with `việc âm` leave a recurring record of matters he deliberately refused to touch?

No hidden letter to Khanh. No `grandfather predicted everything`. No final-boss hint.

## Volume 1 closing image / Khanh's first record

Near the end, Khanh starts a private note/table with simple fields such as:

- name;
- date;
- source;
- what seems certain;
- what remains uncertain.

He records Case Zero, then Case 02.

He does not number them as `Case Zero` / `Case 02` in manuscript. Those remain development labels.

The visual/structural parallel between his digital notes and grandfather's paper notebooks is intentional but should not be narrated as inheritance or chosen-heir symbolism.

Final scene should remain quiet and concrete rather than aphoristic or cliffhanger-heavy.

## Horror distribution

Volume 1 should feel spiritually/horrifically alive beyond its investigations through:

- Case Zero uncanny evidence and marketplace/CCTV residue;
- roughly 6–10 embedded oral ghost stories of varying length;
- Case 02 digital uncanny;
- the direct strongly uncanny short-haunting encounter;
- cultural/ritual atmosphere;
- grandfather's refusal-note pattern.

No monster escalation, exorcism requirement or non-human taxonomy is needed in Volume 1.

## Cultural distribution

- Votive-paper life remains recurring beyond Case Zero because it is the family business.
- Thờ Mẫu / Đạo Mẫu material may enter through Thủy and living cultural scenes, not as a horror-possession mechanic.
- Include at least one ordinary, respectfully researched Buddhist-related scene/context that is not used as an anti-ghost solution.
- Rằm tháng Giêng / Kinh Bắc festival-season texture should appear naturally without turning chapters into tourism exposition.

## Embedded-story working placement

Not hard schedule, but current rhythm:

- Ch.2–3: Lan — gentle uncanny story;
- Ch.7–8: Mai/logistics night-driver story;
- Ch.12–13: darker Lan or family-mutated story;
- Ch.18: Nhân story;
- Ch.20–21: workplace urban legend;
- around Ch.27: another dead-worker/digital oral story;
- Ch.34–40: short haunting contains its own conflicting oral versions;
- Ch.41–43: Thủy or cultural story;
- Ch.43–45: Quân's mutated online retelling.

## Thematic coherence — `Những chuyện tưởng đã kết thúc`

- Case Zero: a thirty-year-old family truth thought buried.
- Case 02: a dead person's identity/traces remain active in an institutional system.
- Short haunting: death did not entirely end a promise, memory or relationship.
- Grandfather: matters he once closed/refused still survive on paper.

This creates thematic unity without requiring conspiracy.

## Khanh's Volume 1 movement

Beginning:
> If enough data exists, there should be an explanation.

After Case Zero:
> It is possible to know almost everything and still not know one essential thing.

After Case 02:
> Uncertainty is not confined to hometown religion, folklore or old people.

After Short Haunting:
> Sometimes the humane thing to do does not depend on proving whether a ghost exists.

Ending:
> If he cannot explain something, he can at least record accurately what is known and what is not.
